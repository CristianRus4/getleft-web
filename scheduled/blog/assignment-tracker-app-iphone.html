Assignment Tracker for Students: An iPhone Setup That Gets Work Started on Time

Updated November 9, 2026 · 7 min read

Most students know when their assignments are due. The syllabus says so, the course page says so, and a planner app will happily list every date for you. Assignments still end up written the night before.

The due date isn't the problem. The gap between "assigned" and "due" is. Three weeks feels like plenty, so nothing happens in week one, a little happens in week two, and week three turns into a scramble. A good assignment tracker is built around that gap: how many days are left, and when you need to start. This guide shows how to set one up on your iPhone, with or without the app we make.

Why a list of due dates isn't enough

A due-date list treats a 10-question problem set and a 3,000-word research paper the same way: one line, one date. Sorted by due date, the problem set sits at the top and the paper waits below it, even when the paper needs starting first.

It also hides time. "Due Dec 4" is a fact you have to do math on. "17 days left" is something you feel. And people routinely underestimate how long work takes (psychologists call it the planning fallacy), so the date you think you can start is usually too late.

The fix is to track one extra date per assignment: the day you have to start.

What to capture for each assignment

Keep it to five fields. Anything more and you'll stop filling them in.

Work backward: the start-by date

The formula is simple:

Start by = due date − work days − buffer

Work days are the days you'll actually spend on it at your normal pace, not hours. Buffer is for everything you didn't plan: a busy week in another class, a cold, a source that takes longer to find. A good default is about a third of the work days, and never less than one day. If you've missed deadlines before, use more.

Sorted by due date, the exam comes last. Sorted by start-by date, it comes before the essay. That reordering is the whole point of working backward. To count days that skip weekends, the school days calculator does the arithmetic.

Split big assignments into checkpoints

Anything with more than three work days gets checkpoints: small, dated pieces you can finish in one sitting. The history essay above, started on the 18th, might look like this:

  • 20th: topic and thesis sentence.
  • 22nd: sources found, outline done.
  • 25th: rough first draft, however bad.
  • 27th: edited and submitted, a day early.

Checkpoints do two things. They make starting easier, because "write a thesis sentence" is less scary than "write an essay". And they tell you early when you're behind. Missing the outline on the 22nd is a warning you can still act on.

A simple assignment system

Four parts. None of them needs a special app.

  1. One capture place. Every assignment goes into the same list the moment it's announced, with the five fields above. Not a screenshot, not the course page, not your memory.
  2. Countdowns for the big items. Papers, projects and exams get a visible countdown, so "days left" is in front of you without opening anything. Small homework doesn't need one.
  3. A weekly review, Sunday, 15 minutes. Add anything new. Set start-by dates. Check which items start this week and which checkpoints are due. If two big things overlap, move a start date earlier now, not later.
  4. A daily study habit. A fixed block, such as "Study 45 minutes after class" on weekdays. The system tells you what to work on; the habit makes sure you sit down. For more on structuring the week, see these time management tips for students.

Questions

What is the best assignment tracker for students on iPhone?

The best setup is usually two apps: one capture list for every assignment, such as Apple Reminders or a student planner like My Study Life, and a countdown app such as Left for the big items. The capture list holds the details; the countdowns keep days left visible so you start on time.

How do I know when to start an assignment?

Work backward from the due date. Subtract the number of days the work will take, then subtract a buffer of about a third of that, and never less than one day. The result is your start-by date.

Should I split big assignments into smaller tasks?

Yes. Anything that needs more than about three days of work should have dated checkpoints, such as outline, draft and edit. Checkpoints make it easier to start and show you early if you're falling behind.

Can Apple Reminders work as an assignment tracker?

Yes, for capturing assignments. Make a list per course, add due dates and times, and use subtasks for checkpoints. It shows due dates rather than days left, so add a second reminder on each start-by date.

Is Left a full student planner?

No. Left has no class timetable or grades. It handles countdowns to big assignments and exams, with checkpoints as todos, plus a daily study habit, and works best next to Reminders or a student planner.
