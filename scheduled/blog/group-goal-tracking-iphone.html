Group Goal Tracking on iPhone: Shared Goals That Stick

Updated January 6, 2027 · 10 min read

Most "group goal" apps are built for work: objectives, key results, owners, status meetings. That's the wrong tool when the group is you and three friends saving for a trip, two people trying to run every day this month, or a couple paying off a card together.

Small groups of people who care about each other need something lighter. One date everyone can see, each person doing their own part, and a way to cheer each other on without turning it into a contest. This guide covers how to set a shared goal well, the three shapes group goals usually take, and how to track each one on iPhone, using Left as the worked example.

Five rules for a shared goal that lasts

Collaborative goal setting goes wrong in predictable ways: the goal is vague, one person carries it, or the visibility turns into pressure. These five rules prevent most of that.

  1. One shared date. The group agrees on a single date: the trip, the race, the exam, the day the card hits zero. Everyone counts to the same day. A goal with four different dates is four goals.
  2. Individual actions. The date is shared, the work isn't. Each person owns something they can do on their own: run three times a week, put a set amount aside each payday, study for 30 minutes. "We'll get fit" has no owner. "I run Monday, Wednesday and Saturday" does.
  3. Light visibility. Friends should see the one thing that matters for this goal, not your whole life. Did you run today? Is the streak still going? That's enough to start a conversation, and little enough that nobody feels watched.
  4. Support, not scoring. Skip public leaderboards that rank people. In a small group, the person at the bottom of the board is usually the one who needed encouragement most, and a ranking tells them they're losing. A quick "you've got this" does more than a position on a list.
  5. Agree what a miss means, up front. Decide together before you start: one missed day is normal, two in a row gets a check-in, nobody gets told off. Saying it out loud early makes the first miss boring instead of awkward.

Keep the group small, too. Two to six people is the range where everyone notices everyone else. Past that, shared visibility becomes background noise.

The three shapes of group goals

Almost every shared goal between friends, partners or family fits one of three shapes. Each needs something different.

1. A date we're all counting to

The goal is an event, and everyone wants the same countdown. What everyone needs is the date in view, so the plan feels real and the bookings get done in time.

In Left, this is a Joint Ahead date: an Ahead countdown shared with one or more friends. The person who creates it is the admin, and once friends accept, the same date appears in everyone's Ahead, with the countdown, the progress since it was set, and a photo. Each person can put it on their own Home Screen or Lock Screen widget. The Joint Ahead dates guide walks through invites, accepting and leaving.

2. Each of us doing the same habit

Two or more people commit to the same action: run every day for 30 days, study every weekday evening, no alcohol in January. Everyone does their own version, and the point is knowing the others are doing it too.

In Left, each person creates their own habit or streak in Since and shares it with the others. A friend who accepts becomes a watcher: for a habit they see whether you complete or miss it, for a streak they see whether it's still going or has restarted, and they can send support or congratulations. Share both ways and you're both watching and both being watched.

3. One person's goal, friends watching

Sometimes the goal belongs to one person and the others are there to back them up. Your sister is quitting smoking. A friend is writing a thesis and wants someone to notice if the daily writing stops.

In Left, that person shares one habit or streak with one or two friends. The friends don't track anything themselves. They see the one item, get a notification if they've turned that on, and send support when it helps. The accountability with friends guide covers what watchers see and how to stop sharing later.

The measurable part: each person's own Aims

Many shared goals also have a number: money saved, kilometers run, debt left, books read. In Left that's an Aim, a countable goal with a unit, a target, a start date and an optional deadline, counting up or down. Aims belong to the person who makes them and aren't shared with friends in the app. The group shares the date and the daily action; each person keeps their own number. If you want to show progress, you can share an Aim as an image card in the chat you already use. The Aims guide covers count-up and count-down goals.

Setting it up in Left

Friends, shared habits and streaks, and Joint Ahead dates are part of Left Premium. Everyone in the group needs Left on an iPhone and a Left account.

  1. Sign in. Open Friends and tap Sign in with Apple. This gives you a profile and a username.
  2. Add each other. In Friends, tap +, then send Your invite link to the group chat, or use Search by @username. Each person accepts the request.
  3. For a shared date: open Ahead, tap + and choose Add a date ahead (or open an existing date). Turn on Friends, tap Add a friend, pick everyone, and save. Invites appear in each friend's Requests until they accept.
  4. For a shared habit or streak: open Since, create the habit or streak (or open one), turn on Friends, tap Add a friend, and save. Friends show as Invited, then Watching once they accept.
  5. For your own number: in Since, tap + and choose an Aim. Set the unit, the target, and a deadline that matches the shared date.
  6. Choose your notifications. In Settings → Friends, pick which friend activity can notify you: completions, misses, support, invites. Turn off anything that starts to feel like nagging.

What friends can and can't see

Adding a friend in Left doesn't open your app to them. Friends only see the items you choose to share with them, one by one.

  • They can see the shared habit (done or missed), the shared streak (going or restarted), and Joint Ahead dates they've joined.
  • They can't see your other habits, streaks, Aims or Ahead dates, or the other friends you've invited to watch an item.
  • You can stop sharing any time. Remove the friend from the item's Friends section. A watcher can also leave on their own, and anyone can leave a Joint Ahead date without deleting it for the others.

Three worked examples

A group trip: four friends, Lisbon in June

One person creates a Joint Ahead date called "Lisbon", from the departure day to the day home, with a photo of the city, and invites the other three. The same countdown now sits in all four Ahead lists. Our post on counting down to a trip with friends has the widget and Live Activity details.

The individual actions are the bookings and the money. Each person owns one job (flights, the apartment, the day trip, the dinner reservation) and agrees a date for it in the group chat. Anyone saving for the trip creates their own Aim, "Lisbon fund", counting up to their own target with the same deadline.

A 30-day running habit between two friends

Sam and Priya want to run for at least 20 minutes a day for 30 days. Each creates a daily habit, "Run 20 min", and shares it with the other. Both accept, so both are watching. When Priya's habit is still unchecked at 7 pm, Sam can send support. When Sam logs a run, Priya can send congratulations. If either of them wants a fixed end, they add a Joint Ahead date for day 30, or for the 5K they've signed up to.

The rule they agree on day one: miss one day, fine; miss two in a row, the other one texts. No ranking, no "you're behind." If you'd rather track a run of days than a ticked habit, a streak works the same way. How to build a streak explains when to use which.

A couple paying off debt

Alex and Jo have 8,000 left on a credit card and want it gone by the end of next year. The shared part is the finish line: a Joint Ahead date called "Card paid off", with both of them in it.

The numbers stay individual. Alex pays from his salary and Jo from hers, so each creates a count-down Aim starting at their half and logs each payment. Once a month they sit down together, compare the two Aims, and adjust if one of them has had a rough month. If one partner wants backup on a spending habit, say a weekly "check the budget" habit, they share it with the other as a watcher.

Common mistakes

  • Turning it into a contest. Comparing totals feels motivating for the person in front and deflating for everyone else. Compare against your own plan, not each other.
  • Sharing everything. Share the one habit or streak that matters for this goal. Five shared items per person in a group of four is twenty things to watch, and nobody watches twenty things.
  • The watcher becomes the manager. A watcher's job is support. If every miss gets a message, the person stops logging honestly.
  • A group goal that's really one person's. If only one of you cares, it's shape three. Set it up that way and the others can support without pretending.

When you need something else

  • Work teams. If you're tracking team objectives, owners and deliverables, use a project management or OKR tool. Left has no team or workspace features.
  • Big public challenges. Running clubs with dozens of members, or a step challenge across an office, suit fitness apps with clubs and challenge boards.
  • Talking. Left has no group chat. Keep the conversation in Messages or WhatsApp, and use Left for the date and the daily check-ins.
  • Working side by side. If a study group needs company while they work, not just visibility afterwards, see our guide to body doubling apps.

Questions

What is a good group goal tracking app for iPhone?

For small groups of friends, partners or family, look for an app that shares a date and individual habits without public rankings. Left does this with Joint Ahead dates and habits or streaks shared with friends, which are part of Left Premium. For work teams, a project management or OKR tool fits better.

How do you set a collaborative goal with friends?

Agree on one shared date, give each person an action they own, share only the one habit or streak that matters, and decide in advance what happens after a miss. Keep the group to two to six people.

Can friends see all my habits in Left?

No. Friends only see the specific habit, streak or Joint Ahead date you share with them, and you can stop sharing at any time. Your other items, including your Aims, stay private.

Is group goal tracking free in Left?

Left is free to download. The free plan includes the Time Left views, the time grid widgets, the wallpaper and 2 countdowns, 2 habits, 2 streaks and 2 Aims. Widgets that show something you create, such as a countdown or a habit, need Left Premium. Friends, sharing and Joint Ahead dates are part of Left Premium, which is a subscription or a lifetime purchase. Prices are shown in the app before you buy.

Do my friends need an iPhone?

Yes. Left is an iPhone app (with an Apple Watch app) and needs iOS 26, and each friend needs Left and a Left account to accept invites.
