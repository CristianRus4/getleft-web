Product Launch Timeline: A 16-Week Template for Small Teams (2027)

Updated January 16, 2027 · 8 min read

A product launch timeline is a plan worked backwards from one fixed date: launch day. For a small team or a solo maker, that plan has to fit on one screen, name concrete tasks instead of departments, and leave room for the things you don't control, like App Store review, a courier or a press embargo.

Below is a 16-week product launch timeline template, from twelve weeks before launch to four weeks after, for an app, a SaaS feature or a physical product. Then come the dates that deserve their own countdown, the mistakes that sink small launches, and how to keep the plan in front of your team.

Start with the launch date, then count back

Pick the date first, even if it feels early: every other deadline hangs off it. Write it at the top of the plan as "L" and express everything else as L minus weeks. "Code freeze, L-3w" is easier to reschedule than "code freeze, March 4".

  • Launch on a working weekday. Many teams pick Tuesday to Thursday, leaving working days on both sides to fix problems.
  • Check the calendar around it. Avoid public holidays in your main market and the days of big industry events where your news would get buried.
  • Count working days, not calendar days. Ten calendar days before launch is often only six or seven working days. The business days from date tool turns "L minus 15 working days" into a real date.

The product launch timeline template (T-12 weeks to T+4 weeks)

Each row has concrete tasks and a test for when the phase is done. Starting with less than twelve weeks? Keep the order and compress the gaps.

How it shifts by product type

  • An app. App Store review is usually quick, but a rejection means a fix and a second review, so submission sits two weeks out. Manual release in App Store Connect lets you get approved early and still launch on your date.
  • A SaaS feature. No store review, so the risk moves to migrations, billing and docs. Ship it behind a feature flag a week early and switch it on at launch.
  • A physical product. Manufacturing, customs and warehouse check-in each add weeks. Treat the date stock is ready to ship as the real launch constraint, and leave at least two weeks of buffer after it.

The four dates worth their own countdown

Only a few dates move everything else when they slip. Give each one its own countdown:

  1. Code freeze or production lock (T-3 weeks). A countdown stops "one more feature" from creeping in.
  2. Review submission (T-2 weeks). The date you hand control to someone else, like Apple, a marketplace or a retailer.
  3. Embargo lift. Usually launch morning, at a set hour in one time zone. If press and team are spread out, check the hour in each city with the time zone meeting planner.
  4. Launch. The date everything else is counted from.

A launch-day runbook in one list

Write it the week before, with times, so launch day is a checklist, not a string of decisions:

  • Release the build, then load the store page yourself on a fresh device.
  • Lift the embargo: publish the blog post and send the waitlist email.
  • Post on your channels, staggered across the day instead of all at once.
  • Check sign-ups, payments and errors every hour for the first six hours.
  • Reply to every comment and email the same day.
  • Note three things to fix tomorrow.

Common product launch timeline mistakes

No buffer for review or shipping

Store review, payment checks and couriers run on their own clocks. If the launch only works when every outside step goes right the first time, it isn't a plan. Put submission at least a week before launch, and stock-ready two weeks before for physical goods.

Launching on a busy news day

Before you lock the date, look for big platform events, holidays and industry conferences in your market that week. Moving a day or two costs little.

No plan after launch day

Most small launches see a spike, then a drop. The rows after launch day in the table, a second push and a review, are there on purpose.

Moving the date without moving everything

If launch slips a week, every milestone slips with it. Written as L minus weeks, that's one change. Written as fixed dates, someone keeps working to the old freeze date.

Questions

How long should a product launch timeline be?

For a small team, 8 to 12 weeks before launch plus about four weeks after is common. A small update can take a few weeks; a physical product often needs months of manufacturing first.

What should a product launch timeline template include?

The launch date, milestones counted as weeks before or after it, concrete tasks per phase, a test for when each phase is done, buffers for store review or shipping, and a post-launch review date.

How far ahead should I submit an app for App Store review before launch?

Many developers submit one to two weeks before launch and set the release to manual. Review is often quick, but leave time for one rejection and resubmission.

How do I count working days to a launch date?
