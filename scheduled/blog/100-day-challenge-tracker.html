100 Day Challenge Tracker: How to Set One Up on iPhone and Actually Finish

Updated November 9, 2026 · 7 min read

A 100 day challenge is long enough to change something and short enough to picture the end. Thirty days is a trial; a year is a resolution you'll forget by March. One hundred days sits in the middle: about three months, one season, long enough for a habit to stop feeling like a project.

Most people who start one don't stop because the challenge was too hard. They stop because day 23 was a bad day, they missed it, and with no rule for what happens next, the whole thing felt broken. This guide covers how to choose a challenge you can finish, how to set rules before you need them, and how to track all 100 days on your iPhone so progress stays visible without effort.

Pick a challenge you can do on your worst day

The challenge has to survive a cold, a late night at work and a weekend away. Ask yourself whether you could do it on day 47 with a headache. If not, shrink it.

  • Make the daily minimum small. "Write 100 words" beats "write a chapter". You can always do more; the minimum is what keeps the chain going.
  • Make it binary. At the end of the day you should know without thinking whether you did it. "Eat better" fails this test; "no sugary drinks" passes.
  • One challenge at a time. Running three 100 day challenges at once triples the number of ways to fail on a bad day.

100 day challenge ideas

Some that work well because they're small, daily and easy to judge:

  • Movement: walk 20 minutes, 50 push-ups, stretch for 5 minutes, take the stairs.
  • Learning: 15 minutes of a language, one chapter of a book, one coding exercise, practice an instrument for 10 minutes.
  • Creating: one drawing, 100 words, one photo, one short video.
  • Quitting: no alcohol, no social media before noon, no takeout, no impulse buys.
  • Mind: 5 minutes of meditation, a one-line journal, in bed by 11 pm.

Notice the two kinds. "Walk 20 minutes" is something you do each day. "No alcohol" is something you don't do. They track differently, which matters for the setup below.

Decide your missed-day rule now

Write the rule down on day 1, while you're calm. Three common versions:

  • Strict: miss a day and you start again at day 1. Motivating for some people, crushing for most.
  • Never miss twice: one missed day is allowed, two in a row resets. This is the rule most people finish with.
  • Count the days, not the chain: the goal is 100 completed days, however long it takes. Slower, but no reset ever.

Whichever you pick, decide what you'll do the morning after a miss. "The next day is just the next day" is a good default.

How to track a 100 day challenge on iPhone

A good tracker answers three questions at a glance: which day am I on, did I do it today, and how much is left. You can cover all three in Left, an iPhone app we make, with three pieces that work together. Any combination of a countdown app and a habit tracker can do the same job.

1. A countdown to day 100

  1. Open Left, go to Ahead and tap +.
  2. Name it ("100 days of running") and set the end date 100 days from your start. The date countdown calculator can work out the exact date.
  3. Add it as a widget so "37 days left" is on your Home Screen or Lock Screen.

A countdown changes how the middle feels. Day 50 of 100 reads as "halfway", not "still 50 to go".

2. A habit or a streak for the daily part

  • For something you do: in Since, tap + and create a habit with a daily period. Each day you tick it off; Left shows the current streak, days completed and missed, your longest streak and a completion grid. Habit strength gives a fairer picture than the streak alone if you're using the never-miss-twice rule.
  • For something you quit: create a streak instead and set Started on... to day 1. It counts up by itself, so there's nothing to tick. If you slip, tap Restart; Left keeps your restarts and longest run.

Mark the milestones

A hundred days is a long, flat road. Break it up:

  • Day 7: the first week is the hardest. Notice that you got through it.
  • Day 25: a quarter done. Check your minimum: is it still small enough?
  • Day 50: halfway. Look back at the completion grid. A few gaps are fine.
  • Day 75: the countdown now reads 25. The end is close enough to sprint.
  • Day 100: decide what happens next: keep the habit with a lighter minimum, raise the bar, or stop on purpose.

Small, immediate rewards work better than one big prize at the end; how to design a reward system explains why. And if a streak is the part that keeps you going, how to build a streak covers the practical side.

When you miss a day

You probably will. Apply the rule you wrote on day 1, log the miss honestly, and do the minimum today. A tracker that shows 94 out of 100 days is a success. One that you abandoned on day 40 because you couldn't face a gap isn't.

Questions

What is a 100 day challenge?

It's a commitment to do something small every day, or to avoid something every day, for 100 days in a row. It's long enough to build a habit and short enough to see the finish line, about three months.

What's the best way to track a 100 day challenge on iPhone?

Combine a countdown to day 100 with a daily habit or streak, and put both on your Home Screen or Lock Screen. In Left, that's an Ahead countdown plus a habit (for something you do) or a streak (for something you quit).

What happens if I miss a day?

That depends on the rule you set at the start. Many people use "never miss twice": one missed day is allowed, two in a row resets. Decide before you begin so a single bad day doesn't end the challenge.

Is 100 days enough to build a habit?

For many simple habits it's a reasonable stretch, but people vary a lot. Treat day 100 as a checkpoint, not a finish line, and keep the habit going with a lighter daily minimum if it's working.

Can I track a 100 day challenge on Apple Watch?

Yes. Left's Apple Watch app lets you log a habit with one tap, check or reset a streak, and add a Habit or Streak complication to your watch face.
