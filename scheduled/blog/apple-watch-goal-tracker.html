Apple Watch Goal Tracker: How to Track Goals From Your Wrist

Updated December 24, 2026 · 7 min read

Apple Watch is already a goal tracker, just a narrow one. It knows how many calories you burned, how long you exercised and whether you stood up this hour, and it closes three rings when you hit your targets. That works because the goal is always on your wrist and the progress updates itself.

The trouble starts with every other goal. Reading 20 books this year, saving for a trip, running 300 km before a race, paying off a card: the watch has no idea about any of them. This guide shows how to turn your Apple Watch into a tracker for those goals too, what kinds of goals work best on the wrist, and how to set it up so progress takes one tap and stays in view.

What Apple Watch tracks on its own

Before adding an app, it helps to know what's built in:

  • Activity rings: Move, Exercise and Stand goals you can change in the Fitness app. Progress fills automatically.
  • Workouts: distance, time and pace per session, saved to the Health app.
  • Health data: steps, sleep, mindful minutes and more, mostly collected without you doing anything.

What's missing is anything you have to count yourself, and any goal with a deadline months away. The rings reset every day; they don't tell you whether you're on pace to run 300 km by March.

Which goals work well on the wrist

The watch is best at the moment just after you do something. So the goals that suit it share three traits: you make progress in small, frequent steps, the step happens away from your desk, and the number matters more than a note about it.

If a goal is vague, the watch can't help. "Get fit" becomes "run 300 km" or "work out 3 times a week". The first is a number goal; the second is a habit. That distinction decides how you track it.

Number goals vs habits vs streaks

  • Number goal: a total you count up or down, with an optional deadline. Books, kilometers, money, weight, debt.
  • Habit: something you do on a schedule. "Work out 3 times a week." You tick it off; the tracker counts how consistent you are.
  • Streak: a counter that grows by itself until you reset it. "No takeout." Nothing to log unless you slip.

Most goal apps handle one of these well. If your goals span all three, one app that handles each saves you three watch apps and three complications.

Setting up a goal tracker on Apple Watch with Left

Left is an iPhone app with an Apple Watch app. Its number goals are called Aims, and they live in Since next to habits and streaks. Here's how to set one up so it works from the wrist. (We make Left; the principles apply to any goal app with a Watch app.)

  1. On iPhone, open Left, go to Since and tap +. Choose an Aim, or start from an Aim template.
  2. Pick count up (books, kilometers, savings) or count down (debt, weight, screen time).
  3. Set the unit and the target. For a count-down goal, set the starting number.
  4. Set the default log amount. This is the key setting for the watch: it's what one tap adds. Make it the size of a typical step, such as 1 book, 5 km or $100.
  5. Add a deadline if the goal has one. Left then shows whether you're on track, ahead, behind or past due.
  6. On the watch, open Left and swipe to Aims. Open the goal and log progress. If you logged by mistake, unlog it.
  7. Add an Aim complication to your watch face and pick the goal it shows. Now the progress sits next to the time.

You choose which screens appear on the watch from the iPhone app, so if you only care about Aims, you can hide the rest. The Aims guide covers every option, and how Aims work has worked examples.

Track goals on Apple Watch

Put the goal on your watch face

A goal you see 80 times a day gets more attention than one inside an app. Complications are how you do that on Apple Watch, and the size you pick changes what you see:

  • Circular: a ring or percentage. Good for one headline goal on a busy face.
  • Rectangular: room for the name and a progress bar. Best when the goal is the point of the face.
  • Inline and corner: a compact number. Good for a secondary goal.

A setup that works for a lot of people: one rectangular complication for the main goal, one circular complication for Habits Today, and a Year complication that shows how much of the year is gone. That last one sounds grim but is useful: a goal for the year reads differently when you can see 85% of the year has passed and you're at 60% of your target.

Keep the goal moving

  • Log right after the action. Finish the run, raise your wrist, tap. Waiting until evening is how weeks go unlogged.
  • Match the deadline to the goal. A year-long goal feels far away until December. Split it into quarters, each with its own Aim, and the pace becomes obvious.
  • Pair a number goal with a habit. "Run 300 km" is the outcome; "run 3 times a week" is the habit that gets you there. Track both: the habit on your watch's Habits screen, the total as an Aim.
  • Review weekly on the phone. The watch is for logging. Once a week, open the goal on your iPhone, check whether you're ahead or behind, and adjust the target or the plan.

For goal-setting itself, SMART goals helps turn a wish into a number, and how to stay consistent with goals covers the weeks when motivation drops.

Other ways to track goals on Apple Watch

  • The Fitness app: perfect for movement goals, but daily only and limited to activity.
  • A habit tracker with a Watch app: fine if your goal is really a routine (see our roundup of goal tracking apps for iPhone).
  • A spreadsheet or notes app: flexible, but nothing you can tap on your wrist, and no progress on the watch face.

Questions

Can Apple Watch track goals other than Activity rings?

Not on its own. The built-in apps track activity, workouts and health data. For goals like books read, money saved or debt paid off, you need an app with a Watch app, such as Left, which tracks number goals called Aims.

How do I log goal progress on Apple Watch?

In Left, open the Aims screen on the watch, open the goal and log progress. Each log adds the default amount you set on iPhone, such as 1 book or 5 km, and you can unlog a mistake.

Can I show a goal on my Apple Watch face?

Yes. Left has an Aim complication in circular, rectangular, inline and corner sizes, and you choose which goal it shows. There are also Habit, Streak, Habits Today and Year complications.

Can I track a goal that counts down, like paying off debt?

Yes. Left's Aims can count up or count down. A count-down Aim starts at a number, such as the balance you owe, and each log reduces what remains.

Is Left's goal tracker free?

Left is free to download, and the free plan includes 2 Aims plus 2 habits, 2 streaks and 2 countdowns, counted over everything you've ever created. Left Premium removes the limit. Prices are shown in the app before you buy.
