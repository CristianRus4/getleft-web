Habit Tracker Ideas: 60 Habits to Track, Sorted by How You Track Them

Updated December 27, 2026 · 9 min read

Most lists of habits to track are sorted by life area: health, mind, money. That helps you think of ideas, but it skips the question that decides whether a habit survives its second week: how do you track it? "Drink water" tracked as one daily checkbox tells you nothing. "Go to the gym" tracked daily makes every rest day look like a failure. "No alcohol" tracked as a daily tick asks you to log something you didn't do, every night, forever.

So this list is sorted by tracking type first, then by life area. Each idea comes with a minimum version: the smallest amount that still counts on a bad day. Pick from the group that matches how the habit actually behaves, and the tracker stops fighting you.

The five ways to track a habit

  • Daily yes/no: you either did it today or you didn't. One tap.
  • Count per day: you do it several times a day and want to reach a number.
  • Weekly target: you want it done a few times a week, on whichever days work.
  • Quit streak: something you stop doing. The counter should run by itself.
  • Number goal: a running total toward a target, like books per year. This isn't really a habit, and treating it like one causes trouble.

1. Daily yes/no habits

The classic checkbox. These work best when the minimum is so small you can't argue with it, and when the answer at bedtime is obvious.

Health

  • Floss: one tooth minimum.
  • Stretch: one minute minimum.
  • Walk outside: to the end of the street and back.
  • Take your vitamins or medication: as prescribed; set a reminder rather than a minimum.
  • Lights out by a set time: in bed, screen down, within 15 minutes of it.

Mind

  • Read: one page minimum.
  • Journal: one sentence.
  • Meditate: three breaths with your eyes closed.
  • Practice a language: one lesson or five new words.
  • Practice an instrument: pick it up and play one scale.

Work

  • Plan tomorrow: write the top one task.
  • Inbox to zero: clear just today's new mail.
  • Write: 50 words on the project.

Home

  • Make the bed: pull the cover straight.
  • Clear the sink: dishes in the dishwasher before bed.
  • Ten-minute tidy: two minutes minimum.

Money

  • Log your spending: enter today's purchases, even if it's zero.

2. Count habits done several times a day

If you do it more than once a day, one checkbox hides the detail you care about. Track it as a count instead: the habit fills up with each tap, and it's complete when you hit the number. Keep the target realistic; you can raise it later.

Health

  • Water: 8 glasses; minimum 4.
  • Posture breaks: stand and roll your shoulders 5 times a day; minimum 2.
  • Short walks: 3 walks around the block; minimum 1.
  • Steps toward a target: log each 2,000 steps as one tap, up to 5; minimum 2.
  • Fruit or vegetable portions: 5 a day; minimum 3.

Mind and work

  • Focus sessions: 4 blocks of 25 minutes; minimum 1.
  • Deep breaths: a breathing pause 3 times a day; minimum 1.
  • Phone-free hours: 3 hours with the phone in another room; minimum 1.

Relationships

  • Reply to messages: 3 people you owe a reply; minimum 1.
  • Say thanks: thank 2 people specifically; minimum 1.

3. Weekly-target habits

Some habits shouldn't happen every day. The gym needs rest days, meal prep happens on Sunday, and calling your parents daily would worry them. Track these with a weekly target, done on any days in the week, so a rest day never shows up as a miss.

Health

  • Gym or strength training: 3 times a week; minimum session is a 15-minute workout.
  • Run or cycle: 2 times a week; minimum 10 minutes.
  • Yoga or mobility class: once a week; minimum a 10-minute video at home.
  • Cook dinner at home: 4 nights a week; minimum is anything with a pan.

Relationships

  • Call family: once a week; minimum a five-minute call.
  • See a friend: once a week; minimum a coffee or a walk.
  • Date night: once a week; minimum a meal without phones.

Home and money

  • Meal prep: once a week; minimum is lunches for two days.
  • Laundry: twice a week; minimum one load.
  • Weekly review of money: once a week; minimum is checking last week's spending.

Mind and work

  • Weekly planning: once a week; minimum three priorities for the week.
  • Learn a skill: 3 sessions a week; minimum 15 minutes each.
  • Side project: 2 sessions a week; minimum open the file and change one thing.

The same logic stretches to monthly habits: deep-clean the fridge once a month, back up your photos once a month, review subscriptions once a month.

4. Quit streaks that count up by themselves

Habits you stop don't need a daily tick. A tracker that asks "did you not drink today?" every night turns quitting into a chore. A streak counter that starts on the day you quit and counts up by itself is simpler: you only touch it if you slip. The minimum here is different. It's not a smaller version of the habit; it's a rule for what counts as a slip, decided in advance.

Health

  • No alcohol: decide up front whether one drink at a wedding resets it.
  • No smoking or vaping: any puff resets.
  • No sugary drinks: coffee with milk is fine, soda isn't.
  • No caffeine after 2 pm.
  • No snacking after dinner: water and tea are allowed.

Mind

  • No doomscrolling before bed: phone out of the bedroom after a set time.
  • No social media before noon.
  • No news first thing: nothing until after breakfast.
  • No nail biting.

Money

  • No takeout: groceries and planned meals out are fine.
  • No impulse buys: anything unplanned over a set amount waits 48 hours.

Counting days since you quit also works for things that aren't habits at all, like days since your last migraine or your last argument about the dishes. The days since widget page shows how that looks on a Home Screen.

5. Number goals that are really Aims, not habits

"Read 24 books this year" sounds like a habit, but it's a total. Tracked as a daily checkbox, it either nags you on days you didn't finish a book or tells you nothing about whether you're on pace. Track it as a number that counts up (or down) toward a target with a deadline. Pair it with a daily habit if you like: "Read 1 page" is the habit, "24 books" is the goal it feeds.

  • Books read: 12 or 24 a year.
  • Distance: 100 km run or walked per month.
  • Money saved: toward a set amount for a trip or an emergency fund.
  • Debt paid off: counting down to zero.
  • Study hours: 50 hours before an exam.
  • Pages written: 300 pages of a draft.
  • Workouts completed: 150 in a year.
  • Screen time: daily average counting down toward a target.
  • Items decluttered: 365 things out of the house this year.

If you track weight as a number, keep the target one your doctor agrees with, and log it weekly rather than daily so normal day-to-day swings don't decide your mood.

How to choose 1–3 to start

Sixty ideas is a menu, not a plan. Start with one, two at most, three only if they're tiny. More than that and a bad week knocks out everything at once.

  1. Use the "sad to drop" test. Read the list and imagine each habit already working. Which one would you be sad to give up after a month? That's the one to start. The ones you feel you should do can wait.
  2. Set the worst-day minimum. Picture yourself sick, late home and tired. What's the least that still counts? Write it next to the habit. If you can't imagine doing even that, shrink it again.
  3. Match the tracking type. Check which of the five groups it belongs to. A weekly habit forced into a daily one fails on the first rest day.
  4. Attach it to something you already do. Floss after brushing. Plan tomorrow when you close the laptop. Read when you get into bed.
  5. Add the next one when the first is boring. Boring means it's automatic. That's usually a few weeks in, sometimes longer.

If you miss a day, the general rule is never miss twice. How to build a streak covers the rest of what keeps a run going.

Questions

What are good habits to track?

Good habits to track are small, specific and easy to judge at the end of the day, like "read one page", "drink 8 glasses of water" or "gym 3 times a week". Pick ones you'd genuinely miss if you dropped them, not ones you feel you should do.

How many habits should I track at once?

Start with one to three. Add another only when the first feels automatic, which is often a few weeks in. Tracking ten at once means one bad week can break all of them.

Should every habit be tracked daily?

No. Exercise, meal prep and calling family usually work better with a weekly target done on any days, so rest days don't count as misses. Habits you're quitting work better as a streak that counts up by itself.

What's the difference between a habit and a goal like "read 24 books"?

A habit is something you repeat on a schedule; a goal like 24 books is a running total with a target. Track the total as a number that counts toward the target, and pair it with a small daily habit such as reading one page.

What should I do when I miss a day?

Do the minimum version the next day and don't miss twice in a row. A tracker that shows most days done is a success, even with gaps.
