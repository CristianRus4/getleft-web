9 Widget Themes for iPhone You Can Build in iOS 26

Updated December 4, 2026 · 9 min read

Search for widget themes and you mostly find packs: a wallpaper, fifty matching icons and a few decorative widgets, sold as a set. They look great in the preview. On your own phone, with your own apps, they often look like someone else's Home Screen, and a week later you're scrolling past a quote widget you've stopped reading.

A theme isn't really a thing you download. It's a small set of rules: one palette, one font feel, one wallpaper, and a few widgets that agree with each other. Once you know the rules for a look, you can rebuild it in twenty minutes with whatever apps you already have. And the setups that still look good a month later have one more thing in common: the widgets show something you actually want to see.

Below are nine theme recipes. Each one gives you the palette, a wallpaper idea, which iOS 26 icon style to use (Default, Dark, Clear or Tinted), and which widgets fit. Mix and adapt; the point is the rules, not the exact colors.

9 widget theme recipes

1. Minimal monochrome

The easiest theme to get right, and the hardest to get bored of.

  • Palette: off-white, mid gray and near-black (#F4F4F2, #8A8A8A, #1C1C1E). No accent at all.
  • Wallpaper: a flat light gray, or a black-and-white photo with lots of empty sky or wall.
  • Icons: Tinted with the color pulled all the way toward gray, or Clear in its light version.
  • Widgets: a year progress grid, a plain calendar, and one countdown. Monochrome suits dot grids and numbers; it punishes anything with a colorful photo.

2. Warm neutrals (latte)

Cream, coffee and caramel. It reads cozy without being cute.

  • Palette: cream, milky beige and a mid brown (#F3E9DC, #D8C3A5, #8B6B4E).
  • Wallpaper: a close-up of linen, paper, a wooden table or a coffee cup shot from above.
  • Icons: Tinted in a soft tan, or Default if you only keep a few apps on the page.
  • Widgets: a habits list with each habit in brown or caramel, a month grid, and a photo widget limited to a warm-toned album.

3. Pastel

Pastels work when you pick two of them, not six.

  • Palette: one pastel pair, such as lilac and peach, or mint and baby blue, plus white.
  • Wallpaper: a soft gradient between your two colors. Gradients hide the edges of widgets, which keeps the page airy.
  • Icons: Clear (light) so the gradient shows through, or Tinted in the darker of the two pastels.
  • Widgets: countdowns to things you're looking forward to (a trip, a birthday), and a habit widget per color. Pastel themes are where it's worth giving each habit its own color.

4. Dark mode with one accent

Almost everything is black or charcoal, and one color is reserved for what matters.

  • Palette: black, charcoal (#1C1C1E) and one bright accent such as orange, electric blue or lime.
  • Wallpaper: pure black, or a very dark photo. On an OLED iPhone a black wallpaper also makes widgets look like they float.
  • Icons: Dark.
  • Widgets: keep everything neutral except one widget in the accent color, such as the countdown to your next deadline or today's habits. The accent becomes a signal: if it's orange, it's the thing to look at.

5. Sage and nature

Muted greens and earth tones, for a page that feels like a walk outside.

  • Palette: sage (#A3B18A), deep moss (#3A5A40) and warm stone.
  • Wallpaper: leaves, ferns or a misty forest, cropped so the top third is calm.
  • Icons: Tinted in sage. It turns a messy mix of app colors into one quiet layer.
  • Widgets: a habits list for outdoor or health habits ("Walk 20 minutes", "Water the plants"), a streak counting days without something, and a weather widget.

6. Clear glass

The look iOS 26 made possible: icons and widgets turn to glass and the wallpaper does all the color work.

  • Palette: whatever your wallpaper is. The theme is the glass.
  • Wallpaper: a photo with soft, large shapes: a sunset, blurred city lights, sand dunes. Fine detail like text or foliage behind glass looks smudged.
  • Icons: Clear, light or dark depending on the wallpaper's brightness.
  • Widgets: ones with bold shapes and few words: a large day or year grid, a big countdown number, a calendar. Small text gets hard to read on glass. For the steps, see how to make widgets transparent.

7. Retro terminal

Green or amber on black, like an old computer screen.

  • Palette: black and phosphor green (#33FF66), or black and amber (#FFB000).
  • Wallpaper: pure black, or a subtle scanline or grid texture.
  • Icons: Tinted in the same green or amber, on a dark appearance.
  • Widgets: anything that's mostly numbers and grids. A monospaced date or clock widget from a customizable widget app, a minute or hour progress grid, and a countdown in days. Dot grids look right at home here.

8. Seasonal: autumn and winter

A seasonal theme is a base theme with the palette shifted, not a rebuild.

  • Autumn palette: rust, mustard and deep brown. Wallpaper: fallen leaves or a foggy street. Icons: Tinted in rust.
  • Winter palette: icy blue, white and slate. Wallpaper: snow, frost or a pale winter sky. Icons: Clear (light) or Tinted in slate.
  • Widgets: a countdown to the holiday, trip or end of term you're waiting for, and a year grid that shows how much of the year is left. Late in the year, that grid is a useful nudge.

Keep the layout the same between seasons and change only the wallpaper, icon tint and widget colors. Your muscle memory survives, and the page still feels new.

9. Calm productivity

A theme built around what you want to notice, styled just enough to feel deliberate.

  • Palette: soft white or light gray, navy, and one muted accent like terracotta.
  • Wallpaper: a plain color or a very soft gradient. Nothing that competes with the widgets.
  • Icons: Default or Tinted in navy, with only four to eight apps on the page.
  • Widgets: the four that earn their place on most people's first page: how much of the day or year is left, today's habits, the next date that matters, and today's calendar.

Build any theme with 4 rules

Every recipe above follows the same four rules. Use them to invent your own.

  1. Start from the wallpaper and take your colors from it. Pick two or three colors that already appear in the image. Widgets and icons that borrow from the wallpaper look planned; colors brought in from nowhere look pasted on.
  2. Use one icon style for the whole page. Default, Dark, Clear or Tinted, not a mix. It's the single setting that does the most to make a page look themed, and it costs nothing.
  3. Pick one font feel and stick to it. Rounded, serif or monospaced. Set the Lock Screen clock font to match, and when a widget app lets you choose a font, choose the same feel every time. Two type styles on one page is the limit.
  4. Every widget has to show something that changes. A countdown, a grid that fills in, today's habits, the next calendar event. Static decoration is the first thing you stop seeing. If you use an accent color, give it to the widget that matters most.

Where theme packs and widget apps fit

You don't need a theme app, but each kind solves a different part of the job:

  • Customizable widget apps such as Widgetsmith let you style date, time, weather, calendar and photo widgets with your own fonts and colors. They're good for the decorative layer. See Widgetsmith alternatives for others in this category.
  • Pre-designed widget apps such as Color Widgets give you ready-made widget designs you recolor or put a photo behind. Fast, but the widgets mostly show the time, date or battery.
  • Theme apps in the style of WidgetClub sell complete packs: wallpaper, icons and widgets in one look. They're the quickest route to a finished screen, usually with a paid tier or subscription for the full packs.
  • Custom icon packs work through the Shortcuts app: each icon is a shortcut that opens the real app. The trade-off is that Shortcuts flashes up first on every tap, and the custom icon doesn't show notification badges. Fine for a few apps, tiring for a whole page. The iOS 26 Tinted and Clear styles get you most of the same unity without the delay.

Questions

How do I get widget themes on my iPhone?

Pick a wallpaper, take two or three colors from it, set one icon style for the page in Home Screen Edit then Customize, and add widgets whose colors you can match. Theme apps sell ready-made packs, but you can build most themes with built-in settings and a couple of widget apps.

What is the best widget theme for iPhone?

The best theme is one you keep using, which usually means a simple palette and widgets that show something useful. Minimal monochrome, dark mode with one accent, and Clear glass are the easiest to get right and the slowest to get boring.

Can I change the color of all my app icons at once?

Yes. In iOS 26, long-press the Home Screen, tap Edit, then Customize, and choose Tinted. Pick a hue and every icon and widget takes on that color.

Do custom app icons slow down my iPhone?

They slow down opening apps slightly. Custom icons are shortcuts, so the Shortcuts app appears briefly before the real app opens, and the icons don't show notification badges.

Do Left's widgets match the Clear and Tinted icon styles?

Yes. iOS applies the Clear and Tinted looks to Home Screen widgets too, and in Left you choose each widget's background and a color for each habit, streak, Aim or countdown.
