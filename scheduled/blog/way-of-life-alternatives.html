7 Best Way of Life Alternatives for iPhone in 2026

Updated November 13, 2026 · 9 min read

Checked 20 October 2026. Every competitor detail below comes from that app's US App Store page and Apple's public lookup data, fetched that day. Left details come from the app itself.

Way of Life is one of the oldest habit trackers on the iPhone, first released in 2010, with 4.8 stars from 5,317 ratings on the US App Store. You "Mark each day green or red, keep your chains going", add notes, and read your trends in charts. People look for an alternative when they want more free habits, an Apple Watch or iPad app, a visual grid, or something more like a journal. Here are seven alternatives, including ours, and when each one fits.

Disclosure: we make Left, so we have put it first. Here is why, and when another app suits you better.

Quick verdict

  • Best for habits on the Home Screen and Lock Screen, next to countdowns and year progress: Left.
  • Best simple yes-or-no grid with 4 free habits: HabitKit.
  • Best "don't break the chain" board with skips, on every device: everyday.
  • Best for habits and goals together, with templates: Strides.
  • Best classic checklist with skips and one upgrade: Habit List.
  • Best if you use Way of Life as a journal: Daylio.
  • Best one-time purchase with Apple Watch: Streaks.

What Way of Life does well

By Way of Life ApS. Its App Store page describes a daily journal where you mark each habit green or red, with chains, red chains for streaks of bad days, pie charts and bar graphs with trend lines, a scoreboard, notes, tags, reminders "with flexible schedules and custom messages", themes, a color-blind mode, widgets, Siri and Shortcuts, and export. Its release notes mention Yes, No and Skip buttons.

  • Price: free to download. "No maze of paywalls. Everything above is included. Premium simply unlocks unlimited habits." The listing doesn't say how many habits are free, and its Premium rows don't state a period, though its 2023 release notes say "Subscriptions introduced."
  • Platforms: iPhone, Apple silicon Macs and Vision Pro.
  • Recent changes: backup and restore became free for everyone in February 2026, and all themes went free in 2025. The latest version came out in October 2026.
  • Privacy: its privacy label lists only data not linked to you, and the listing says it "doesn't pry into, sell, or share your data".

If you like its red-and-green journal, there's little reason to leave. The alternatives below differ on free habits, devices, layout and price.

1. Left: habits where you'll see them

Left is a time awareness app for iPhone. Habits live in Since, next to streaks and countable goals (Aims), with countdowns in Ahead and the time left in the day, week, month and year in Time Left. Way of Life is built around a journal you open, with widgets too. Left puts your habits on the Home Screen and, with a rectangular widget, on the Lock Screen.

  • Log from the Home Screen: the interactive Habit widget lets you tap today to log it, without opening the app. The Habits list widget shows 4 or 8 habits, and a rectangular Habit widget sits on the Lock Screen.
  • Flexible schedules: daily, weekly or monthly goals; every day, weekdays, weekends, specific days, or a number of days per week.
  • Reminders: up to 4 per habit, each with its own days and time, plus a persistent Live Activity an hour before your chosen time if the habit still isn't logged.
  • Progress: days completed, days missed, longest streak, a completion grid and habit strength.
  • Streaks for bad habits: a streak counts up from the last time, and Restart keeps your average and longest run.
  • Left Premium extras: unlimited habits, Apple Health tracking that completes a habit when you meet the goal, and friends who can hold you accountable.

Best for: a few habits you want to see and tick off all day, alongside countdowns and how much of the year is left.

Price: free to download, with no ads. The free plan includes 2 habits (plus 2 streaks, 2 Aims and 2 countdowns), counted over every habit you've ever created, so deleting one doesn't free a slot. Left Premium, a subscription or lifetime purchase, unlocks unlimited habits. Prices are shown in the app before you buy.

Pros: a free interactive Home Screen habit widget and free Lock Screen widgets; Live Activity reminders; habits, streaks, goals and countdowns in one app.

Cons: only 2 free habits; built around widgets rather than a journal you browse; no Apple Watch app; iPhone only, with iOS 26 or later.

2. HabitKit

By Sebastian Roehl. Its App Store page shows each habit as a grid of tiles: "Check a day off with a simple yes or no, count reps, or measure amounts", add a short note to any day, set streak goals and read monthly charts. It has a quit-a-habit mode and backs up to iCloud once a day.

Best for: Way of Life users who want the same daily yes or no as a colorful grid.

Price: "HabitKit is free to use with up to four habits, including streaks, statistics, notes, and reminders." Pro, monthly, yearly or lifetime, adds unlimited habits, Home Screen widgets and more reminders.

Pros: 4 free habits with notes; no ads and no account; a lifetime option.

Cons: widgets need Pro.

3. everyday

By Everyday Growth S.L. Its App Store page is built around "DON'T BREAK THE CHAIN": all your habits on "a single beautiful board", a "skip option" for days off, a "no weekends" setting, a "break habit" mode with descending colors, current and longest streaks, and apps for the web, browser extensions, iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch, kept in sync.

Best for: chain lovers who want the same board on every screen they use.

Price: free to use, with an optional everyday Premium upgrade, offered yearly, monthly or as a lifetime purchase. The listing doesn't say what's free.

Pros: skips without breaking the chain; web and browser extensions; Apple Watch.

Cons: free limits aren't stated; widgets aren't mentioned.

4. Strides

By Goals LLC. Its App Store page offers four tracker types (streak, milestone, average and target), more than 150 templates, progress reports, notes on logs, unlimited reminders and Apple Health integration, with an Apple Watch app.

Best for: tracking goals and targets as well as daily habits.

Price: free to use for the habits that matter most; Strides Plus, monthly, yearly or lifetime, adds unlimited trackers, sync, tags, a Today list and export.

Pros: 4.8 from 19,242 ratings; goals and habits in one; Apple Health and Watch.

Cons: sync and export need Plus; the free tracker limit isn't stated; widgets appear only in release notes.

5. Habit List

By Gerard Gualberto. Its App Store page describes streak badges, the option to "Skip habits without breaking your streak" or mark one as missed, flexible schedules by specific days, any days or intervals, weekly and monthly trends, and a passcode or Face ID lock, with no account needed.

Best for: a plain checklist with skips and one upgrade.

Price: "Your first three habits are on us." One Premium upgrade adds unlimited habits, the lock and dark mode.

Pros: 3 free habits; skips; a single upgrade.

Cons: rated 4.0, the lowest here; no updates between January 2024 and February 2026; widgets aren't mentioned.

6. Daylio

By Relaxio s.r.o. Its App Store page describes a "micro-diary": pick your mood, add activities, and see your year as "Year in Pixels". It adds daily, weekly or monthly goals, achievements, a PIN lock, PDF and CSV export and a calendar widget.

Best for: people who use Way of Life's notes as a diary and want a journal first, habits second.

Price: free to try; Daylio Premium is listed at several price points. The listing doesn't say what's free.

Pros: 4.8 from 62,103 ratings; journaling and goals together; a Year in Pixels view.

Cons: a mood journal rather than a habit tracker, so chains aren't the focus; the listing doesn't say what's free.

7. Streaks

By Crunchy Bagel Pty Ltd. Its App Store page lets you "Track up to 24 tasks you want to complete each day", record daily notes, add negative tasks to break bad habits and link tasks to Apple Health, with iCloud sync, Home Screen widgets and an Apple Watch app with complications.

Best for: paying once and logging from your wrist.

Price: a paid download, with no in-app purchases listed.

Pros: one price; Apple Watch, iPad and Mac; its privacy label lists only diagnostics, not linked to you.

Cons: no free version to try; up to 24 tasks.

Questions

What is the best alternative to Way of Life?

HabitKit is the closest in spirit, with a simple yes-or-no grid and 4 free habits. everyday suits you if you want a chain board with skips on every device, Strides if you track goals too, and Left if you want your habits on the Home Screen and Lock Screen.

Is Way of Life free?

It's free to download. Its App Store page says Premium "simply unlocks unlimited habits" and that everything else is included, but it doesn't say how many habits are free.

Which Way of Life alternatives work on Apple Watch?

everyday, Strides and Streaks list Apple Watch. Way of Life, HabitKit, Habit List, Daylio and Left don't list an Apple Watch app.

Which habit trackers let you skip a day without breaking the streak?

everyday has a skip option that doesn't break the chain, and Habit List lets you skip habits without breaking your streak. Way of Life's release notes also mention a Skip button.

Can I log a habit from my iPhone Home Screen?

Yes. Left's interactive Habit widget lets you tap today's habit to log it from the Home Screen, for free. HabitKit's widgets come with Pro, and Streaks lists Home Screen widgets.
