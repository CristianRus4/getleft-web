How to Customize Your iPhone Home Screen in iOS 26

Updated December 7, 2026 · 11 min read

You look at your Home Screen dozens of times a day, usually for a second or two on the way to something else. Most people never set it up on purpose. Apps land wherever iOS puts them, page two fills with things you opened once, and the first page ends up as a grid of red badges.

iOS 26 gives you more control than any version before it: icon styles, apps placed anywhere on the grid, resizable widgets, pages that change with your Focus mode. This guide walks through each of those with concrete steps, then ends with the part most guides skip: deciding what your first page is for.

Before you start: clear the clutter

Customizing a crowded Home Screen just gives you a nicer-looking crowd. Spend five minutes trimming first.

  1. Long-press an empty spot on the Home Screen until the icons jiggle.
  2. For any app you rarely open, tap the minus on its icon and choose Remove from Home Screen. The app stays installed and moves to the App Library.
  3. Drag the apps you use every day toward the bottom of the screen and into the dock, where your thumb reaches them.
  4. Tap Done.

A good test: if you can't say why an app is on the first page, it doesn't need to be there. You can still find it in seconds by swiping down to search or swiping left to the App Library.

Wallpaper and Lock Screen pairing

On iPhone, the Lock Screen and Home Screen wallpapers come as a pair. You set them up together, and you can keep several pairs and switch between them.

  1. Long-press the Lock Screen (unlock with Face ID first, but don't swipe up), then tap Customize, or + to start a new one.
  2. Edit the Lock Screen: photo or color, clock style, and widgets under the clock.
  3. Choose the Home Screen side of the pair. You can use the same image, a blurred version of it, a plain color or gradient, or a different photo.

You can also do this from Settings > Wallpaper.

A practical tip: the busier your Home Screen, the calmer its wallpaper should be. A detailed photo looks great on the Lock Screen, where only the clock and a few widgets sit on top of it. Behind a full grid of icons, the same photo turns into noise. Blurring the Home Screen side, or using a solid color picked from the photo, keeps the pair matched while letting icons and widgets stay readable.

If you want your Lock Screen to show something that changes on its own, such as how much of the year has gone, the Left Wallpaper guide shows how to set a wallpaper that updates itself through the Shortcuts app.

Change how app icons look

iOS lets you restyle every icon and widget on the Home Screen at once.

  1. Long-press an empty spot on the Home Screen.
  2. Tap Edit in the top corner, then Customize.
  3. Pick a style from the panel at the bottom of the screen.

The styles:

  • Default: the colorful icons each app ships with.
  • Dark: darker icon backgrounds that are easier on the eyes at night. Apps that provide a dark icon use it; others are darkened automatically.
  • Clear: glass-like icons and widgets that let the wallpaper show through. This is the most striking look in iOS 26, and the one where wallpaper choice matters most.
  • Tinted: every icon in a single color you choose, with a picker for the hue and its strength.

Some styles can follow your iPhone's light or dark appearance, or stay fixed in one. The same panel has a size option. The larger setting makes icons and widgets bigger and hides the app names under them, which looks clean and is easier to tap, but only works if you know your apps by their icons. If you use Tinted or Clear, that gets harder, since many icons end up looking alike.

Widgets follow these styles too. For the steps to make widgets see-through, see how to make widgets transparent.

Put apps anywhere on the grid

For years, iPhone apps filled from the top-left and snapped together. Now you can leave empty space.

  1. Long-press an empty spot until the icons jiggle.
  2. Drag an app to any free cell on the grid. It stays there, with gaps around it.
  3. Tap Done.

Apps still sit on the grid, so you can't place an icon between two cells. But you can now keep the top half of a page empty to show off a wallpaper, line apps up along the bottom edge, or frame a widget with space around it.

Gaps are useful for more than looks. A Home Screen with a few apps around the bottom and nothing at the top is easier to use one-handed, and it means the first thing your eye lands on is the wallpaper or a widget rather than another icon.

Hide pages and use the App Library

You don't have to delete a Home Screen page to get it out of the way. You can hide it and bring it back later.

  1. Long-press an empty spot until the icons jiggle.
  2. Tap the row of dots above the dock. iOS shows all your pages side by side.
  3. Uncheck a page to hide it. Drag pages to reorder them.
  4. Tap Done.

Hidden pages are perfect for seasonal setups (a travel page you only want during trips) or for a cleanup you're not sure about yet.

The App Library is the last page, past all your Home Screens. It holds every installed app, sorted into automatic categories, with a search field at the top. To stop new apps from landing on your Home Screen, go to Settings > Home Screen & App Library and choose to keep newly downloaded apps in the App Library only. Once you do, nothing appears on your Home Screen unless you put it there.

You can also lock an app behind Face ID, or hide it so it only appears in a hidden folder at the bottom of the App Library. Long-press the app icon to find those options.

Widgets: add, resize, stack and edit

Widgets are where the Home Screen goes from a launcher to something that tells you things. The basics:

  • Add one: long-press an empty spot, tap Edit, then Add Widget. Search for an app, swipe through its widget sizes, and tap the one you want.
  • Sizes: small (the space of four app icons), medium (eight), and large (sixteen). In edit mode you can drag a widget's corner handle to change its size, and some app icons can turn into a widget straight from their long-press menu.
  • Stacks: drag a widget onto another of the same size to stack them. Swipe up or down on the stack to flip between them. One stack of three small widgets uses the space of one.
  • Smart Stack: a stack that iOS rotates for you, showing what it thinks you need at that time of day. Long-press it and tap Edit Stack to reorder widgets or turn off automatic rotation and suggestions.
  • Edit a widget: long-press it and tap Edit Widget to choose what it shows: which countdown, which habit, which city's weather.
  • Interactive widgets: some widgets have buttons or checkboxes you can tap without opening the app, such as ticking off a to-do, playing a podcast, or logging a habit.

Our step-by-step guide to adding widgets on iPhone covers the Lock Screen, StandBy and troubleshooting in more detail.

Two rules of thumb. First, a Smart Stack is good for things you might want and bad for things you must see; if a widget matters, give it a fixed spot. Second, more than three or four widgets on one page and they stop registering. You end up scrolling past them like a feed.

Different Home Screens for different Focus modes

Apple's Focus modes (Work, Personal, Sleep, Do Not Disturb and any you create) can each show their own Home Screen pages. Turn on Work and your iPhone shows only the work page; turn it off and your regular pages return. This is an iOS feature and has nothing to do with Left's Focus timer, which is a Pomodoro-style timer inside the app.

  1. Go to Settings > Focus and pick a Focus.
  2. Find the option to customize screens and choose which Home Screen pages appear while that Focus is on.
  3. Optionally, link a Lock Screen to the same Focus from the Lock Screen gallery.
  4. Add a schedule so the Focus turns on by itself at set times or places.

A simple version: a work page with your calendar, mail and the next deadline, and an evening page with books, music and nothing that buzzes. If you want a countdown or habit widget that changes with each Focus, this guide to Left widgets per Focus mode walks through it.

Custom app icons with Shortcuts

If the four icon styles aren't enough, you can make an icon from any image using the Shortcuts app.

  1. Open Shortcuts, tap + to make a new shortcut, and add the Open App action.
  2. Choose the app you want to open.
  3. Use the shortcut's share or details menu to add it to the Home Screen.
  4. Give it a name and tap the icon to choose a photo or image as the new icon.
  5. Remove the original app icon from the Home Screen so you don't have two.

The trade-off is real: tapping the custom icon runs a shortcut, so Shortcuts flashes up first before the real app opens. You also lose notification badges on that icon. It's fine for a handful of apps you want to match a theme. For a whole Home Screen, it adds a small delay to every tap, every day. Design your first page around what you want to notice

Most Home Screen advice stops at looks. But your first page is the one screen you see every time you unlock your phone, often without meaning to. That makes it valuable space. Instead of asking "what looks good here?", ask "what do I want to notice every day without going looking for it?"

For most people the answer is a short list:

  • Time: how much of the day, week or year is left. Not to stress you out, but because time feels endless until you see it.
  • Today's habits: the two or three things you've decided to do daily, and whether you've done them yet.
  • The next date that matters: a trip, an exam, a deadline, a birthday.
  • One streak: something you're quitting or keeping up, counting up by itself.

Everything else, including most apps, can live on page two or in the App Library. The first page becomes a dashboard you glance at, not a menu you dig through.

Keep it working after the first week

  • Change something when you stop seeing it. A widget you've stopped noticing isn't doing its job. Move it, resize it, or swap it for something new.
  • Retire finished things. When a countdown ends or a habit sticks, take it off the first page so the next one gets your attention.
  • Turn off badges you don't act on. In Settings > Notifications, switch off badges for apps whose red dot you ignore anyway. A calm first page stays calm.
  • Keep one page for one purpose. If the first page is for noticing, resist adding "just one more" app to it.

Questions

How do I customize my Home Screen on iPhone?

Long-press an empty spot on the Home Screen until the icons jiggle. From there you can move apps anywhere on the grid, add widgets, hide pages, and tap Edit then Customize to change the icon style. Wallpaper is set together with the Lock Screen, from the Lock Screen gallery or Settings.

How do I make my iPhone app icons bigger without names?

Long-press the Home Screen, tap Edit, then Customize, and choose the larger icon size. Icons and widgets get bigger and the app names underneath disappear.

Can I leave empty spaces on my iPhone Home Screen?

Yes. In iOS 26 you can drag apps to any cell on the grid and leave gaps around them. Icons still snap to the grid, so you can't place one between two cells.

How do I hide a Home Screen page on iPhone?

Long-press the Home Screen, tap the dots above the dock, and uncheck the page you want to hide. Tap Done. Its apps stay installed and the page comes back when you check it again.

Do custom icons made with Shortcuts slow down opening apps?

Slightly. A custom icon runs a shortcut, so Shortcuts appears briefly before the app opens, and the icon doesn't show notification badges. It's fine for a few apps but adds up if you replace every icon.

Is iOS Focus mode the same as Left's Focus timer?

No. Apple's Focus modes are an iOS setting that silences notifications and can switch your Home Screen pages. Left's Focus is a Pomodoro-style timer inside the app and doesn't change your Home Screen layout.
