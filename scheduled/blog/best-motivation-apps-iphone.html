Best Motivation Apps for iPhone (2026), by What Moves You

Updated November 9, 2026 · 9 min read

Search for a motivation app and you get a mix: habit trackers, quote widgets, focus timers, coaching programs. They aren't doing the same job. Each one pulls a different lever, and the one that works for you depends on what has kept you going in the past.

So this list is grouped by lever, not ranked. Start with the diagnostic, then go to the group that fits.

Disclosure: we make Left, so it appears in the groups where it fits. It isn't a quotes or coaching app, and in that group we point you to apps that suit the need better.

Which lever do you need?

Think of the last thing you kept up for a month or more. What kept you going? Pick the line that sounds most like you.

  • "I liked watching the number go up." You run on visible progress. See Seeing progress.
  • "I had a date to work toward." A deadline or an event pulls you forward. See Something to look forward to.
  • "I didn't want to break the run." Consistency itself is the reward. See Not breaking the chain.
  • "Someone was expecting me." You show up for other people more easily than for yourself. See Other people.
  • "I needed a nudge, or a plan to follow." The hard part is starting, or knowing what to do. See Words and coaching.

Two lines fit? Most people have a main lever and a backup. Use one app for the main lever and a widget for the backup, rather than installing five apps. If nothing has ever lasted a month, start with the smallest version of the chain: one habit, every day, on your Lock Screen.

1. Seeing progress: goal and habit trackers

This lever works when the gap between where you are and where you're going is visible. The trick is to track something you can add to today, not only the end result.

"Get fit" gives you nothing to watch. "Run 300 km this year" moves every time you run, and by March you can see whether you're on pace.

Left: Aims and habits you can see

Left's Aims are goals you can count: a unit, a target, a start date and an optional deadline. They count up, such as books read, money saved, km run or hours studied, or down, such as debt left or screen time. You log with one tap using a default step, or type an amount, and the progress history shows how it moved. Habits sit alongside for the repeated actions that feed a goal, on a daily, weekly or monthly schedule or on specific days.

What makes it fit this lever is where the progress shows up. Dozens of Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets keep your goals in view, and habit widgets show what's still unchecked today, so you see where you stand without opening the app.

Best for: goals with a number, kept in sight all day.

Track a goal you can see

Strides

Strides is a goal and habit tracker built around targets. Alongside daily habits, you can set a total to reach by a date and see whether you're ahead of or behind where you need to be.

Best for: detailed goal tracking, where staying on pace is what keeps you going.

Habitify

Habitify is a habit tracker with a clean daily list and progress statistics, and it isn't limited to iPhone, which helps if you track on more than one device.

Best for: people who are motivated by completion rates over weeks and months.

2. Something to look forward to: countdowns

Some people don't move for a goal, they move for a date. A trip in 40 days makes the savings, the packing list and the training feel real.

The practical move is to put the date where you look, and attach the small actions to it. "Half marathon in 62 days" on your Lock Screen does more than a calendar entry you scroll past.

Left: Ahead countdowns

Left's Ahead counts down to dates: trips, birthdays, exams, deadlines and events. Each one shows progress from the day you set it to the date, with a photo and reminders, and the final countdown can run as a Live Activity. You can also save Ahead dates to your calendar.

Pair an Ahead date with an Aim for the work that leads up to it, and you pull two levers at once: "Japan in 90 days" next to "Saved 1,200 of 3,000".

Dedicated countdown apps do this too. Apple Calendar holds the date, but won't show the days left at a glance.

3. Not breaking the chain: streaks

A streak turns consistency into something you don't want to lose. After ten days, day eleven is less about the habit and more about protecting the run. It can backfire: a broken streak makes some people give up entirely.

Two rules help. Pick a schedule you can keep on a bad day, and decide in advance what happens after a miss, such as "never miss twice". Our guide to building a streak on iPhone walks through both.

Streaks

Streaks is a long-standing habit tracker for iPhone and Apple Watch. It keeps your list short on purpose and puts each habit's run front and center. It's a paid app.

Best for: people who want one focused streak tracker with Apple-style design.

Left: habits and streaks

Left has two kinds of chain. Habits are actions you mark done on their schedule, and some can need more than one completion per period. Streaks count up by themselves from a start date or your last reset, which suits "days without" goals such as days without alcohol or days since you started. Habit widgets show what's still unchecked today, so a run doesn't end just because you forgot.

Forest

Forest applies the chain to focus. You start a session and a tree grows; leave to check another app and it withers. It suits people whose motivation drops the moment the phone comes out.

Left also has Focus, a Pomodoro-style timer that's part of Left Premium, with a strip of today's sessions and a focus history. It doesn't block apps, so if staying off your phone is the real problem, Forest is the better fit.

4. Other people: accountability

For many people the strongest lever isn't inside them. It's knowing someone will notice. Keep it light: one person, one habit, and a simple rule such as "if I miss two days, you ask me why". There's more on this in how to stay consistent with goals.

Left: Friends

Friends is part of Left Premium. You add a friend by invite link or @username, then choose a specific habit or streak to share. Your friend sees whether you complete it or miss it, and can send support. For shared plans, a Joint Ahead date puts the same countdown in each person's Ahead. Here's how to set up accountability with friends, step by step.

Best for: one or two people you trust, watching one habit each.

Habitica

Habitica turns habits and to-dos into a role-playing game. You can join a party with friends, so skipping your dailies affects the group as well as you. It suits people who are motivated by games and a little friendly pressure.

Some apps go further and ask you to put money on a goal. That works for some people and feels punishing to others, so try a friend first.

5. Words and coaching: quotes and routine apps

Sometimes what's missing is the nudge or the plan, not the tracking. A good line in the morning can reframe the day, and a guided routine removes the "what do I do first?" problem. Be honest about this lever: a quote feels good for a minute, so it works best paired with one concrete action.

Left isn't a quotes or coaching app. If this is your main lever, one of these will suit you better. You can still add a habit widget for the action the quote is meant to start.

Fabulous

Fabulous is a routine coach. It guides you through building a morning routine and other habits in small steps, with coaching content along the way. It's a subscription app.

Best for: people who would rather be told what to do next than design their own system.

Motivation, and other daily quote apps

Daily quote apps such as Motivation show you a short quote each day, often grouped by theme. They won't track whether you did anything, so use them as the spark, not the system.

How to combine levers without app overload

One main app, one backup lever, and widgets where you look. A worked example for someone training for a race:

  • Goal: run a 10K in March.
  • Main lever, the date: an Ahead countdown to race day, on the Lock Screen.
  • Backup, progress: an Aim for km run, with a target of 200, logged after each run.
  • The chain: a "Run" habit on Monday, Wednesday and Saturday, so a quiet Tuesday never breaks anything.
  • Other people: share the habit with one friend who's training too.

That's one app and a couple of widgets. If coaching is your lever, swap the Aim for a routine app and keep the countdown.

If motivation is hard because of ADHD, rewards that land quickly matter more than distant goals, and our ADHD reward system guide covers how to set that up. If low motivation lasts for weeks and affects your sleep, appetite or mood, talk to a doctor: an app can help with habits, but it isn't a treatment.

Questions

What is the best app for motivation on iPhone?

It depends on what moves you. For visible progress, try a goal or habit tracker such as Left, Strides or Habitify; for streaks, Streaks or Left; for accountability, Left's Friends or Habitica; for daily nudges, a coaching app like Fabulous or a quotes app.

Left is free to download. The free plan includes the Time Left views, the widgets, the wallpaper and 2 countdowns, 2 habits, 2 streaks and 2 Aims. Left Premium is a subscription or a lifetime purchase. Prices are shown in the app before you buy.

Do motivational quote apps work?

They can give you a short lift, but they don't track whether you acted on it. They work best paired with one concrete action, such as a habit you tick off the same day.

How do I stay motivated after breaking a streak?

Restart the same day and treat the old run as proof you can do it. Decide in advance on a rule like "never miss twice", and choose a schedule you can keep on a bad day.

Can a friend see my habits in Left?

With Friends, part of Left Premium, you choose a specific habit or streak to share. Your friend sees whether you complete it or miss it, and can send support.
