Habit Reminder App for iPhone: How to Set Reminders You Won't Ignore

Updated December 15, 2026 · 7 min read

Most habit reminders die the same way. A notification arrives at 8:00 pm saying "Read for 20 minutes". You're cooking. You swipe it away, fully intending to read later. Later never comes, and after a week of swiping, your thumb clears that notification before your brain even reads it.

The reminder wasn't wrong; it was badly timed, easy to dismiss and blind to whether you'd already done the thing. A good habit reminder app on iPhone fixes all three. This guide covers how to set reminders that get acted on, and what to look for in an app that sends them.

Why habit reminders get ignored

  • Wrong moment. A fixed time ignores what you're doing. 8:00 pm is fine on Monday and useless on Friday.
  • Too easy to dismiss. A banner is gone with one swipe. Once dismissed, there's nothing to bring you back.
  • No memory. The app reminds you even when you already did the habit, which teaches you its reminders don't matter.
  • Too many. Ten habits with ten reminders each is a notification feed, and feeds get ignored.

Five rules for reminders that work

1. Tie the reminder to a routine, not a clock

Habits stick best when they follow something you already do: after coffee, after you park, after you brush your teeth. Set the reminder for just after that moment. "Stretch" at 7:10 am, when you usually finish breakfast, works better than "Stretch" at 9:00 am, when you're already at work.

2. Different days, different times

Weekdays and weekends rarely look alike. A reading reminder at 9:30 pm on weeknights and 3:00 pm on Sundays matches real life. A good app lets each reminder have its own days.

3. Remind once, then escalate only if needed

The first reminder should be gentle. A second, stronger nudge should come only if the habit still isn't done as the day closes. That's the difference between a reminder and nagging.

4. Make acting on it one tap

If the reminder says "Drink water" and logging it takes opening an app and finding the habit, you'll do neither. The habit should be loggable from the Home Screen, the Lock Screen or your watch.

5. Fewer habits, fewer reminders

Two or three habits with well-timed reminders beat ten with generic ones. Habits that are already automatic don't need reminders at all; turn theirs off.

Apple's Reminders app is great for one-off tasks and recurring chores, and you can tick items off from its widget. But it doesn't track a habit over time: there's no streak, no history and no sense of whether the habit is getting stronger. For that, you want a habit tracker with good reminders built in. Setting up habit reminders in Left

Left is an iPhone app we make for habits, streaks, countdowns and seeing how much time is left. Its habit reminders are built around the rules above.

  1. Open Left, go to Since and tap +. Type the habit ("Read", "Stretch", "Take vitamins") to pick a template, or choose Build from scratch.
  2. Set the schedule: every day, weekdays, weekends, specific days, a number of days per week, or a weekly or monthly target.
  3. Turn on Get reminders. Add up to 4 reminders, each with its own days and time. For example: 7:10 am on weekdays, 9:00 am on weekends.
  4. Turn on Get a persistent reminder and set Before what time?, such as 10:00 pm. If the habit still isn't logged, a Live Activity appears on your Lock Screen an hour before that time. If you've already done it, nothing appears.
  5. Add the Habit widget to your Home Screen and set its layout to Text, so you can tap today's circle to log without opening the app.

The persistent reminder is the piece most apps don't have. It's your end-of-day backup, and because it only shows up when the habit is still undone, it never trains you to ignore it. The habit tracker page shows the widgets and reminder options.

Beyond notifications

The strongest reminder isn't a notification at all; it's seeing the habit where you already look. A few ways to keep habits in view:

  • Lock Screen: a Habits list widget shows today's progress every time you pick up the phone. See the Lock Screen widget guide.

Questions

What is the best habit reminder app for iPhone?

Look for a habit tracker that lets you set several reminders per habit with their own days, reminds you only when a habit isn't done yet, and lets you log from a widget. Left does all three, including a persistent reminder that appears only if the habit is still unlogged.

Can Apple Reminders be used as a habit tracker?

Partly. Reminders can repeat daily and you can tick them off, but it doesn't keep a streak, a history or any measure of consistency. A dedicated habit tracker shows whether the habit is actually sticking.

How many reminders should I set for a habit?

Usually one well-timed reminder plus an end-of-day backup. More than that tends to turn into noise. Habits that are already automatic often need no reminder at all.

How does Left's persistent reminder work?

You choose a time with Before what time?. If the habit still isn't logged an hour before that time, Left shows a Live Activity on your Lock Screen. If you've already logged it, nothing appears.

Can I set different reminder times on weekends?

Yes. In Left you can add up to 4 reminders per habit, each with its own days and time, so weekday and weekend reminders can differ.
