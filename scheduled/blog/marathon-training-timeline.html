Marathon Training Timeline: Week by Week from Race Day (2027)

Updated December 10, 2026 · 7 min read

A marathon training timeline is easiest to build backwards. Put race day on the calendar first, count back the weeks your plan needs, and the start date, the long runs and the taper fall into place. This guide answers how many weeks to train for a marathon, explains the four phases, and gives an 18-week timeline you can map onto your own race date.

How many weeks to train for a marathon

Most marathon plans run 12 to 20 weeks. Which end of that range fits you depends on where you start, not on how fast you want to finish.

Where you are todayTypical plan lengthTotal runway to race day
Little or no regular running16 to 20 weeks, after a base-building periodOften 6 to 12 months
Running a few times a week for several months, first marathon16 to 20 weeks4 to 5 months
Has run a marathon before and kept running since12 to 16 weeks3 to 4 months

Starting from little running? The first job is making three or four runs a week feel routine. Many first-marathon plans assume you can already run about 5 to 6 miles (8 to 10 km) without stopping.

Add one or two spare weeks on top. A cold or a work trip will cost you a week at some point, and a buffer lets you repeat a week instead of skipping ahead.

Work backwards from race day

Here's the method, using an 18-week plan and a spring race on Sunday, April 25, 2027:

  1. Fix race day. Sunday, April 25, 2027 is the last day of week 18.
  2. Count back 18 weeks. Week 1 starts on Monday, December 21, 2026.
  3. Add the buffer. With two spare weeks, start on Monday, December 7, so the holidays don't eat into the plan.
  4. Mark the phase changes. Taper starts Monday, April 5. The longest run falls on the weekend of April 3 and 4.

The four phases of marathon training

Base (weeks 1 to 6)

Mostly easy running. The goal is consistency: the same number of runs each week and a long run that grows slowly.

Build (weeks 7 to 12)

Weekly distance rises, the long run gets longer, and many plans add one quality session a week, such as a tempo run. A half marathon tune-up often sits near the end.

Peak (weeks 13 to 15)

The biggest weeks, usually including one or two runs of about 18 to 20 miles (29 to 32 km). Rehearse race day here: shoes, clothes, breakfast and fuel.

Taper (weeks 16 to 18)

Volume drops over about 2 to 3 weeks while some faster running stays in, so you arrive rested rather than unfit. Sluggish legs and restlessness during the taper are common.

How the long run grows

In general terms:

  • It starts at a distance you can already run comfortably.
  • It grows by about 1 to 2 miles (2 to 3 km) every week or two.
  • Every third or fourth week is a cutback week with a shorter long run, so your body can absorb the work.
  • It peaks around 3 weeks before race day. Most plans for first-time marathoners stop at about 20 miles (32 km) and leave the full distance for race day.

Dates worth their own countdown

Race day isn't the only date that matters. These tend to sneak up:

  • Registration. Popular races can sell out or run an entry lottery months before race day. Check the race website for the opening date and any deadline.
  • Plan start. The Monday of week 1, or of your first buffer week.
  • Tune-up race. About 6 weeks out, if you're doing one. It needs its own registration too.
  • First 30 km long run. About 5 weeks out. It shows whether your shoes, fueling and pacing hold up.
  • Taper start. About 3 weeks out.
  • Race-week logistics. Travel, a place to stay, bib pickup (often a day or two before the race), the start time and how you'll get to the start.

When the timeline slips

Few training blocks go exactly to plan. A few general rules help:

  • Don't make up missed runs. Pick the plan back up where you are, rather than doubling the next week.
  • Use the buffer. If you lose a week in the base or build phase, repeat a week instead of jumping ahead.
  • Lost several weeks? A slower target or a later race beats racing undertrained.

Pain that changes how you run, or doesn't ease with a few days of rest, is a reason to see a doctor or physical therapist, not to push through.

Questions

How many weeks do you need to train for a marathon?

Most plans last 12 to 20 weeks: 16 to 20 for a first marathon if you already run regularly, 12 to 16 if you're experienced. Starting with little running, allow several months of base building first.

Is 12 weeks enough to train for a marathon?

It can be for runners who have done a marathon before and are running steadily. For a first marathon, 16 to 20 weeks is the safer choice.

How long is a marathon taper?

Usually 2 to 3 weeks. Weekly distance comes down gradually after the longest run while some shorter, faster running stays in, so you reach race day rested.

What is the longest run before a marathon?

Many plans for first-time marathoners peak at about 18 to 20 miles (29 to 32 km), around 3 weeks before race day. Most don't include a full 26.2 mile run before the race itself.

How long does it take to train for a marathon from scratch?

Starting from little or no running, plan on roughly 6 to 12 months: a few months to build a regular running habit and a comfortable 5 to 6 mile run, then a 16 to 20 week marathon plan. Check with a doctor before starting if you have health concerns.
