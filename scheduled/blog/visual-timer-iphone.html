Visual Timer on iPhone: Free Options and the Shapes That Work (2027)

Updated January 17, 2027 · 7 min read

A visual timer shows time left as a shape that shrinks, like a colored wedge, a row of squares or a bar, instead of a string of digits. You can get partway to a visual timer on iPhone for free with the Clock app, and all the way with an app built around shapes. This guide covers who it helps, which shapes work and why, what the built-in options can and can't do, and a worked setup.

The short version: if you need a timer now and then, the Clock app and StandBy are enough. If you lose track of time, work in focus blocks, or need a timer that a child or a whole room can read at a glance, you want a bigger shape.

What a visual timer is

A digital timer asks you to do a little math: is "17:42" a lot for this task? A visual timer skips the math. You see that about a third of the disc is left, and you know it in under a second.

The idea is simple: the area that is left stays in proportion to the time that is left. The best-known physical version is the red disc made popular by Time Timer in classrooms. On a phone, the shape can be bigger, change color, and follow you to the Lock Screen.

A good one shows proportion (how much of the whole is gone) and reads in one look from across a desk. That glanceability is why visual tools tend to beat feature-heavy ones; more in why visual beats feature-heavy.

Who a visual timer helps

  • Kids and transitions. "Ten more minutes at the park" means little to a five-year-old. A wedge getting smaller does. Explain it once: "when the color is gone, we put on shoes."
  • ADHD and time blindness. Many people with ADHD describe time as "now" or "not now", with little in between. A timer you can always see turns the time that has passed into something you notice. See ADHD time blindness and how widgets help.
  • Focused work. A visible end makes a hard task easier to start and easier to stop. This is the core of the Pomodoro method, covered in our Pomodoro timer for iPhone page.
  • Classrooms. Turns, reading time, clean-up. Mirror the phone to the classroom display so the back row can see it.
  • Meetings. A 15-minute stand-up with a draining shape on the table keeps everyone brief without anyone calling time.

A timer is a tool, not a treatment. If you think you or your child has ADHD, talk to a doctor or psychologist.

The shapes that work, and why

Disc or wedge

The classic. It looks like a clock face, so almost everyone reads it untaught, and it reads well from a distance. Best for a few minutes up to an hour; past that, a 60-minute dial needs a second lap that looks like the first.

Draining squares

One square per minute, and one square disappears each minute. The count of squares left is the number of minutes left, so you get the glance of a shape and the precision of a number. Squares also help with time estimation: "How many squares did getting dressed take?"

A bar

Bars fit narrow places like widgets and the Lock Screen, and suit long spans such as a workday. They are weak for short timers: on a 60-minute bar, one minute moves it less than 2%.

Color and glow

Color alone doesn't show how much time is left, but it shows the phase from across a room: one color for work, another for a break. Pair it with one of the shapes above.

Free visual timer options already on your iPhone

Here is what iOS 26 gives you for free.

  • Clock timer. Open Clock, tap Timers, pick a duration and tap Start. While the app is open, a ring drains around the digits. When you leave the app, the countdown moves to a Live Activity on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island.
  • The timer in Control Center. Touch and hold the Timer control to set a duration with a slider and start it without opening Clock. If it's missing, add it from Control Center's edit mode.
  • StandBy. Charge your iPhone on its side while it's locked. A running Clock timer shows up as a Live Activity, and you can tap it to fill the screen.

Tips that make any visual timer work

  • Explain the shape first. A moving shape is only a cue if you know what it means.
  • Put it where you already look. A desk stand or a Lock Screen widget beats a timer buried in an app.
  • Go shorter than you think. A 15-minute block that gets started beats a 50-minute block that doesn't.
  • Watch for pressure. If a draining shape makes someone anxious, try a calmer style, hide the numbers, or use the timer only for breaks.

Questions

Does iPhone have a built-in visual timer?

Not a dedicated one. The Clock timer shows a thin ring around large digits, and a running timer appears on the Lock Screen, in the Dynamic Island and in StandBy. For a big wedge or draining squares, you need a timer app.

What is the best free visual timer for iPhone?

For minutes, start a Clock timer and put your iPhone in StandBy. For the day, month or year, Left's Time Left grid widgets are free. See the year progress widget.

Do visual timers help with ADHD?

Many people with ADHD find that seeing time pass makes it easier to start, stop and switch tasks. It is a support, not a treatment; for diagnosis or treatment, talk to a doctor or psychologist. More in how to focus with ADHD.

Can I put a visual timer on my iPhone Lock Screen?

Yes. A running Clock timer shows there as a Live Activity. Left's Focus adds its own Live Activity and a Lock Screen widget; the rectangular size has Start and Pause buttons.

How long should a visual timer be for kids?

Short. For transitions, 2 to 10 minutes is common; for homework, many families start with 10 to 15 minutes and a short break. Ask your child's teacher or therapist for advice that fits your child.
