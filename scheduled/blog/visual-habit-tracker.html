Visual Habit Tracker: Which View Keeps You Going, and How to Set It Up on iPhone

Updated December 2, 2026 · 7 min read

A list of habits with checkboxes tells you about today. A grid of filled squares tells you about the last three months in one glance: the good weeks, the holiday gap, the slow restart, the run that's going now. That's the whole case for a visual habit tracker. You don't read it; you see it, and seeing a pattern you built is a stronger nudge than any reminder.

But "visual" covers very different views, and they don't all motivate the same way. A ring that fills each day suits a habit you do several times a day. A year grid suits a slow habit you'll keep for months. A single streak number suits some people and crushes others. This guide walks through the main views, which habits each one fits, and how to set up a visual habit tracker on iPhone so the view is on your Home Screen, not buried in an app.

Why seeing progress works

  • It shows effort you'd otherwise forget. Thirty logged days blur together in memory. Thirty filled squares don't.
  • It makes gaps tolerable. One empty square in a mostly full row looks like what it is: one day. A streak counter reset to zero says the opposite.
  • It gives a reason to log. Filling the next square is a tiny reward, and it happens right after the action, which is when rewards work best.
  • It works at a glance. No numbers to interpret. You know how you're doing before you've thought about it.

The main views, and what each is good for

ViewWhat it showsBest forWatch out for
ChecklistToday: done or notMany small daily habitsNo sense of history
Month calendarEach day this month, filled or emptyHabits with a monthly rhythm, reviewsResets every month
Year grid (heatmap)Every day of the year as a squareLong-term habits: reading, practice, exerciseLittle to see in the first weeks
Ring or fillProgress toward today's countHabits done several times a day (water, breaks)Says nothing about yesterday
Streak numberDays in a rowQuitting something; competitive peopleOne miss resets it to zero
Strength or consistency lineHow regular the habit has been latelyAnyone who misses days and needs a fair scoreLess dramatic, so less of a thrill

The trick is to combine two: one view for today (checklist or ring) and one for the long run (grid or strength). Today's view gets you to log; the long view keeps you going.

Paper or app?

Paper trackers in a bullet journal are lovely and they work: coloring in a square with a pen is a satisfying ritual. Their weakness is that the journal is closed most of the day. An app's weakness is the opposite: it's always with you, but hidden inside an icon unless you put it somewhere visible.

The answer for most people is an app with widgets. The grid or ring sits on the Home Screen or Lock Screen, where you see it dozens of times a day, and logging is one tap. If you love paper, keep the journal for the monthly review and let the phone do the daily part.

How to set up a visual habit tracker on iPhone

Here's how to do it in Left, an iPhone app we make, which shows habits as grids, streaks and a habit strength score, with widgets for each. The same idea works with any tracker that has widgets.

  1. Create the habit. Open Left, go to Since, tap + and type what you want to do to find a template, or choose Build from scratch. Pick a color: it's the color your squares fill with, so choose one you like looking at.
  2. Set the count. For a habit done several times a day ("Drink water, 6 times"), each tap fills a bit more. For weekly or monthly habits, pick the period and how many times.
  3. Look at the habit's page. You'll see the current streak, days completed and missed, your longest streak, a completion grid of past days, and habit strength.
  4. Put it on the Home Screen. Touch and hold an empty spot, tap Edit, then Add Widget, search for Left and add the Habit widget. Touch and hold it, tap Edit Widget and choose the habit. In the Text layout you can tap today's circle to log without opening the app.
  5. Add the overview. The Habits list widget shows several habits and their progress at once, up to 8 in the large size.
  6. Add the Lock Screen. A circular or rectangular Habits list widget on the Lock Screen shows today's progress without unlocking.

Get the visual habit tracker

Pick the right view for the habit

  • "Read 10 pages": a daily habit you'll keep for a year. Look at the grid weekly; let the widget handle today.
  • "Drink water, 6 times a day": the fill matters more than history. A Habit widget on the first page, tapped through the day.
  • "Gym, 3 times a week": a weekly habit. Streaks count weeks you hit the target, so a rest day isn't a miss.
  • "No alcohol": don't make it a checkbox. A streak that counts up on its own, shown on a Streak widget, is the clearest visual for quitting. See the days since widget.
  • "Save $2,000": not a habit at all. A progress bar toward a number fits better; in Left that's an Aim. See how Aims work.

Make missed days look like what they are

A visual tracker is only motivating if a gap doesn't feel like failure. A few habits help:

  • Look at strength, not just the streak. Habit strength reflects how regular you've been lately, so a single miss nudges it down instead of wiping it out.
  • Set realistic schedules. If you'll realistically run four days a week, set four days a week. Then the other three don't show as misses.
  • Pause instead of breaking. Away for two weeks? Archive the habit. Archived days count as skipped, and the habit comes back when you do.
  • Zoom out. If this week looks bad, look at the grid for the last three months. The pattern is usually better than the week.

For the long game, how to build a streak covers what to do after a miss, and visual goals on your screens applies the same idea to bigger goals.

Questions

What is a visual habit tracker?

A habit tracker that shows your progress as a picture instead of a list: a grid of filled days, a ring that fills up, a streak count or a consistency line. It lets you see your pattern at a glance.

What's the best visual habit tracker for iPhone?

Look for one that shows both today's progress and the long-term pattern, and puts them on your Home Screen as widgets. Left shows a completion grid, streaks and habit strength, with Habit and Habits list widgets on the Home Screen and Lock Screen.

Is a year grid or a streak better for motivation?

It depends on the habit and on you. A streak is exciting but resets to zero after one miss. A year grid keeps every good day visible, which most people find easier to stick with over months.

Can I see my habit tracker without opening the app?

Yes, with widgets. Left's Habit widget shows a habit on the Home Screen and lets you tap today's circle to log it, and the Habits list widget works on the Lock Screen too.

Should I use a paper habit tracker or an app?

Both work. Paper is satisfying but stays closed most of the day; an app with widgets keeps the habit in view. Many people use the app daily and a journal for a monthly review.
