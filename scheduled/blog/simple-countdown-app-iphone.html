A Simple Countdown App for iPhone: What to Look For and a 2-Minute Setup

Updated November 9, 2026 · 7 min read

Most people who search for a simple countdown app for iPhone want one thing: type a date, see how many days are left, and have that number sit on the Home Screen or Lock Screen without fuss. Many countdown apps start there and then pile on themes, stickers, ads and settings until adding a date takes longer than checking a calendar.

This guide is about the short version. What "simple" should mean in a countdown app, the few extras that are worth keeping, a setup you can finish in two minutes, and the habits that keep a countdown useful after the first week. We use Left for the worked example, but the checklist works for any app.

What "simple" should mean in a countdown app

Simple isn't the same as bare. A countdown app with no widget is simple and useless, because you still have to open it. A good test: how many taps from "I have a date" to "I can see the number without opening anything"? Here's what that looks like in practice.

  • Adding a date takes seconds. A name and a date are the only required fields. Time of day, repeats and images are optional.
  • One clear number. "23 days" in a large font beats a dashboard of days, hours, minutes and seconds ticking at once.
  • A widget for the Home Screen and Lock Screen. The point of a countdown is seeing it without trying. If it only lives inside the app, you'll forget it exists.
  • No ads in the way. An ad between you and the number is the opposite of simple.
  • Nothing to set up before your first date. No account wall, no tour you have to sit through, no themes to pick first.
  • Sensible defaults. Days for dates a few weeks out, weeks or months for dates far away, and the option to change it when you want.

The extras worth keeping

A few features look like clutter but save you work later. These are the ones we'd keep in any countdown app:

ExtraWhy it earns its place
Yearly repeatBirthdays and anniversaries roll over to next year by themselves, so you set them once.
Choice of unit"7 weeks" reads better than "49 days" for a trip in two months. "3 hours" beats "0 days" on the day itself.
Import from CalendarYour flight or concert is already in Calendar. Re-typing it is how mistakes creep in.
A photo backgroundA picture of the beach you're flying to makes the widget something you want to look at.
A final-hours viewOn the day, a Live Activity on the Lock Screen counts the hours and minutes without a widget refresh.
ArchiveFinished countdowns move out of the way instead of piling up as "-14 days".

Everything else (sticker packs, dozens of fonts, confetti, social feeds) is a matter of taste. None of it changes how well the countdown does its job.

Set up a simple countdown on iPhone in 2 minutes

Here's the whole flow in Left, where countdowns live in a section called Ahead.

  1. Open Left, go to Ahead and tap Add a date ahead (or +).
  2. Type a name, or search the ready-made dates (holidays and common events). To bring in something already in your calendar, choose From calendar.
  3. Set Starts to the date and, if it matters, the time. For something that lasts, like a trip, set Until as well.
  4. Save. That's a working countdown.
  5. Touch and hold an empty part of the Home Screen, tap Edit, then Add Widget. Search for Left, choose the Ahead widget and a size, then tap Add Widget.
  6. Touch and hold the widget, tap Edit Widget and pick your date.

That's it. If you want more later, it's all optional: Repeat (weekly, fortnightly, monthly or yearly), Add image for a photo or symbol, and Show as… under More options to switch between hours, days, weeks, months and years. Put it where you'll actually see it

A countdown only works if you notice it. Three places do that on iPhone, from most to least glanceable:

  • Lock Screen. You see it every time you pick up the phone, often dozens of times a day. Left's Ahead widget comes in circular and rectangular Lock Screen sizes; see how to add a Left widget to your Lock Screen.
  • First Home Screen page. Top-left is where the eye lands. One countdown per page is plenty.
  • StandBy. If your iPhone charges on its side on a nightstand or desk, the countdown can sit there all night. Our guide to a countdown screensaver with StandBy covers the setup.

For the last stretch, open the date in Left and, under More options, turn on Get ready for the start. In the hours before the date begins, a Live Activity shows the countdown on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, ticking down by the minute.

Keeping it simple with more than one date

Simple gets harder once you have five countdowns. A few rules keep it calm:

  • One headline date. Give the date that matters most the Lock Screen or the medium widget. Everything else can wait inside the app.
  • Use a list widget for the rest. Left's Time Ahead list shows up to 7 dates in one widget, each in its own unit, so a trip in weeks and a deadline in days can sit side by side.
  • Name dates specifically. "Lisbon, May 14" beats "Trip". You'll know what the number means at a glance.
  • Archive finished dates. A countdown that hit zero last week only adds noise.
  • Sort by closest. In Ahead you can sort by closest, farthest or name, so the next thing is always on top. More in how to organise Ahead and Since.

When a countdown app isn't needed at all

Sometimes you only want the answer once. "How many days until June 3?" doesn't need an app: our free date countdown calculator works in the browser and gives days, weeks and months. If you just need a timer for the next 20 minutes, the Clock app's timer is already on your phone. A countdown app earns its spot when the date is far enough away that you'd otherwise forget it, or close enough that you want to watch it arrive.

Questions

What is the simplest countdown app for iPhone?

Look for an app where a name and a date are the only required fields, with a Home Screen and Lock Screen widget and no ads. Left works this way: add a date in Ahead, add the Ahead widget, and pick the date. Repeats, photos and units are optional.

Does iPhone have a built-in countdown to a date?

Not as a widget. The Clock app has a timer for minutes and hours, and Calendar shows events, but neither shows "days until" a date on your Home Screen. For that you need a countdown app or widget.

How do I put a countdown on my iPhone Lock Screen?

Touch and hold the Lock Screen, tap Customize, choose the Lock Screen, tap the widget area and add a countdown app's widget. In Left, add the Ahead widget in the circular or rectangular size, then tap it to choose your date.

Can a simple countdown app show hours instead of days?

Good ones let you choose. In Left, each date has a Show as… setting for hours, days, weeks, months or years, and the Live Activity counts the final hours on the Lock Screen.
