Best Time Management Apps for Students on iPhone (2027)

Updated December 28, 2026 · 9 min read

A student's time problems are specific: a timetable that changes every term, assignments with hard due dates, exams that arrive all at once, a semester that quietly runs out, and study sessions that never start. One app rarely handles all of that, and students who make one app do everything usually stop opening it by week four.

This guide splits student time management into four jobs, recommends apps for each, and shows how to build a small "student stack" on your iPhone: one app per job, four at most.

Disclosure: we make Left, so we have put it first for the job it does best: deadlines and the semester ahead. For the other jobs, we recommend other apps.

The four jobs in a student's time

  1. Timetable and assignments. Where your classes are, which week of a rotating schedule you're in, and what homework belongs to which course.
  2. Calendar. Everything with a time: lectures, shifts, appointments, the group project meeting.
  3. Deadlines and the semester ahead. How many days until the essay, the exam, the end of term, and how much of the month is already gone. Most planners hide this inside a list.
  4. Starting and finishing study sessions. A focus timer that makes "just 25 minutes" easy to begin and gives you a record of what you did.

A test for your stack: from the Lock Screen alone, can you say what's due next and how many weeks of term are left? If not, your deadlines are stored, but not seen.

2. My Study Life

A long-running student planner built around your timetable. You enter your classes, including rotating week schedules, then attach homework, tasks and exams to each course, and it reminds you what's coming.

Best for: students with a complex timetable who want classes, homework and exams in one planner.

Price: free to start, with in-app purchases.

Pros: organized by class, which matches how school actually works; handles rotating schedules.

Cons: you need to enter your whole timetable to get the most from it; deadlines live inside the app's lists rather than in front of you.

3. Power Planner

A student planner for classes, assignments and exams that also tracks grades and works out your GPA. If you want to see how each assignment moves your grade, it's the one to look at.

Best for: university students who track grades and GPA alongside their workload.

Price: free to start, with in-app purchases.

Pros: grades and deadlines in the same place; a clean class-by-class view.

Cons: more setup than a simple list; grade tracking is wasted if your course doesn't need it.

4. School Assistant

A simple planner for assignments, tests and your class schedule. It keeps things light: a list of what's due, with reminders.

Best for: students who want the planner job done with as little setup as possible.

Pros: quick to learn; its App Store listing says it's free, with no ads.

Cons: fewer extras than the bigger student planners.

5. Structured

A visual day planner that lays your tasks and calendar events on one timeline, so you can see the gaps between lectures and fill them with study blocks.

Best for: students who plan best by seeing the shape of the day.

Price: free to start, with in-app purchases.

Pros: makes free time between classes obvious; easy to rearrange a day that went wrong.

Cons: plans days, not semesters; long-range deadlines still need a home elsewhere.

6. TickTick

A general to-do list with a calendar view and a built-in focus timer. It isn't made for school, but plenty of students run their coursework from it, one list per course.

Best for: students who want tasks and a timer in the same app and don't need a timetable.

Price: free to start, with in-app purchases.

Pros: flexible lists; one app instead of two for tasks and focus.

Cons: no class structure; flexible tools reward the students who set them up carefully.

7. Forest

A focus app that grows a virtual tree while you stay off your phone and lets it wither if you leave. It turns a study session into something you don't want to break.

Best for: students whose biggest problem is picking up the phone mid-session.

Pros: simple and motivating; works for any subject.

Cons: does one job; no deadlines or planning.

8. Apple Calendar and Reminders

Already on your iPhone, free, and good enough for the calendar job. Put lectures and fixed events in Calendar, quick to-dos in Reminders, and you don't need a third-party calendar.

Best for: the calendar slot in almost every stack.

Pros: nothing to install; shares with family or study groups through iCloud.

Cons: deadlines become one more event in a busy week, easy to scroll past.

Build your stack: a worked example

Maya is in her second year, with 14 classes a week, a Saturday job, three assignments due before the break and two exams in December. Her stack:

  • Timetable and assignments: My Study Life holds her classes and the homework for each course.
  • Calendar: Apple Calendar holds lectures, work shifts and the group project meetings.
  • Deadlines and the semester: Left has an Ahead date for the statistics exam and one for the last day of term. The rectangular Lock Screen widget shows the exam countdown; a Home Screen widget shows the month draining away.
  • Study sessions: an Aim of 50 study hours by the exam date, logged after each session. With Left Premium, she starts Focus sessions from the widget.

Her Sunday check takes 15 minutes: open the planner, update the week's assignments, move the countdown to whatever is due next, and look at her study hours against the target. If the Aim is behind, she adds two study blocks to the calendar before Monday.

To work out the numbers for your own term, the semester countdown calculator shows the weeks left, and the school days calculator counts only the weekdays you'll actually be in class.

Why most student time apps get dropped

  • Too many apps for the same job. Pick one per job and delete the rest.
  • Deadlines you have to go looking for. If you have to open an app to see what's due, you'll look only when it's urgent. Put the next one or two on a widget.
  • Planning that starts with tasks, not time. Count the hours you actually have between classes, work and sleep before you list what you'll do with them.
  • Timers you have to remember. A focus timer works best when you can start it from the Home Screen in one tap.

Which one should you pick?

  • You know what's due but it always sneaks up on you: Left, with deadlines on the Lock Screen.
  • Your timetable is complicated or rotates: My Study Life.
  • You care about grades and GPA: Power Planner.
  • You want a simple, free planner: School Assistant.
  • You plan best by seeing the day: Structured.
  • You want tasks and a timer in one app: TickTick.
  • Your phone is the problem during study: Forest, or an iPhone Focus mode.
  • You want nothing new to install: Apple Calendar and Reminders.

Most students end up with three: a student planner, Apple Calendar, and Left for what's ahead. Add a focus timer when starting is the hard part; Left's is on the Pomodoro timer page.

Questions

What is the best time management app for students?

There isn't one app that does every job well. A student planner such as My Study Life or Power Planner suits the timetable and homework, Apple Calendar covers fixed events, and Left keeps deadlines, exams and the semester ahead on your Lock Screen.

Is there a free time management app for students on iPhone?

Yes. Apple Calendar and Reminders are built in, and School Assistant says it's free. Left is free to download, and its free plan includes the Time Left views, the time grid widgets and 2 countdowns, enough for your next exam and the end of term. Countdown widgets need Left Premium.

How do I see how many weeks are left in the semester?

Use the semester countdown calculator on our site for a quick answer. To keep it in view, add the last day of term as an Ahead date in Left and put its widget on your Home Screen or Lock Screen.

Do I need a separate Pomodoro app for studying?

Not always. TickTick includes a focus timer, and Left Premium includes Focus, a Pomodoro-style timer with a widget and a Live Activity. If your phone itself is the distraction, Forest or an iPhone Focus mode helps more.

Can I put assignment deadlines on my iPhone Lock Screen?

Yes. In Left, add the deadline as an Ahead date and add the Ahead widget to your Lock Screen. It shows the days left, and the final countdown can run as a Live Activity.
