Event Countdown App for iPhone: Setups That Work (2026)

Updated November 9, 2026 · 7 min read

A good event countdown app for iPhone does one job well: it tells you how long until the thing you're going to, as one number you can read at a glance, without opening anything. A concert, a conference, a wedding: the date is fixed, and the countdown's job is to keep it in view and get you there on time.

Below: what makes an event countdown useful, what Apple's own apps can do, three worked setups, and what to use if you need a countdown on a projector.

What makes a good event countdown on iPhone

Five things matter more than themes and fonts.

One clear number per event

"Concert in 42 days" beats a block of days, hours, minutes and seconds. Give each event its own countdown and a specific name: "Greek Theatre show, Aug 14", not "Concert".

The right unit for the distance

A number that barely moves stops being read. Change the unit as the date gets closer.

How far awayBest unitWhy
More than 6 monthsMonths"8 months" is easy to picture; "243 days" is just a big number.
1 to 6 monthsWeeks"9 weeks" changes once a week, which matches how you plan.
Under a monthDaysEvery day counts now, so every day should change the number.
The day itselfHours"6 hours" tells you when to start getting ready.
The last few hoursLive minutesA ticking count while you dress, drive, park and queue.

Visible without opening an app

A countdown you have to open is a countdown you forget. The Lock Screen is the best spot, since you see it every time you pick up your phone. A Home Screen widget or StandBy come next.

A live countdown for the final hours

Widgets refresh on a schedule iOS controls, which is fine for days and weeks. On the day, you want a Live Activity: a countdown that ticks on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island.

The real start time

Count to the moment that matters: doors at 7:00 PM, the keynote at 9:30 AM, the ceremony at 3:00 PM. A countdown that hits zero at midnight is wrong by most of a day, and the people going with you should see the same time you do.

Keep the event in Calendar anyway: it holds the address and the invitees, and some countdown apps can import it so you don't type the time twice. What Apple's apps don't show is a running count to a date weeks away.

Dedicated countdown apps vary a lot. Check that you can change the unit per event, that there's a Lock Screen widget and a Live Activity, and that the free tier covers your number of events.

Three worked setups

A concert

Tickets bought in March for a show on August 14, doors at 7:00 PM.

  • Name and time: "Greek Theatre show", Starts August 14 at 7:00 PM. If you'll skip the opener, use the headliner's set time instead.
  • Unit: weeks until mid-July, then days.
  • Image: the tour poster or a photo from the last show you saw.
  • Reminder: a week before, to find your tickets and plan the ride home.
  • On the day: Get ready for the start puts a ticking countdown on the Lock Screen while you get ready and travel.

A conference

A three-day conference has two times that matter: when it opens and, if you're speaking, your slot.

  • The conference: import it from your calendar so the time matches the agenda. Starts at the first keynote, Until at the close of the last day. Show it in days.
  • Your talk: a second date, shown in hours, with Get ready for the start on, so a live countdown walks you into your slot. For timing the talk itself, see our presentation timer setup.
  • Free plan fit: these two dates use both free countdowns, so if you'll want more events later, plan for Left Premium.

A wedding

Weddings are long countdowns, often a year or more from the engagement.

  • Time: Starts at the ceremony time, not just the day.
  • Unit: months while it's more than six months away, then weeks, then days for the final month.
  • Image: an engagement photo or the venue.
  • Together: with Left Premium, a Joint Ahead date lets your partner see the same countdown on their phone.
  • The nearer deadline: a second date for the RSVP cutoff or the final vendor payment.

For the planning itself, month by month, see our event planning timeline.

A countdown on a big screen (for organizers)

If you're running the event and want a countdown on a projector or TV, a phone app is the wrong tool. Left is built for your iPhone and its widgets; it has no presentation mode for a room. Use a browser on the laptop driving the screen:

  • Before doors or a keynote: open the date countdown calculator, set the date and start time, and choose minutes or the days, hours, minutes and seconds view. It updates every second.
  • Breaks between sessions: the countdown timer runs a fixed time, such as 15 minutes. It has no alarm sound and stops if you close the tab.
  • Fill the screen: on a Mac, choose View, then Enter Full Screen (Control-Command-F); on Windows, press F11. Zoom in with Command or Control and the plus key. Turn off display sleep and notifications before the session.

For produced keynotes with speaker monitors and a tech booth, use dedicated show-timer software. Keep Left on your own phone for the run-up.

Questions

Does iPhone have a built-in event countdown?

Not for dates weeks away. The Calendar widget shows your upcoming events and a Clock timer counts down up to 23 hours 59 minutes, but neither shows a running "days until" number for an event next month. For that you need a countdown app, or a browser tool such as the date countdown calculator for a quick check.

How do I put an event countdown on my iPhone Lock Screen?

Add the event in a countdown app that has Lock Screen widgets. Then touch and hold the Lock Screen, tap Customize, choose Lock Screen and add the app's widget. In Left, that's the Ahead widget in its circular or rectangular size; our Lock Screen widget guide has every step.

Can an event countdown show hours instead of days?

Yes, if the app lets you choose the unit. In Left, each date has a Show as... setting for hours, days, weeks, months or years, and Get ready for the start adds a live countdown on the Lock Screen before the event begins.

How do I show an event countdown on a projector or big screen?

Use a browser on the laptop connected to the projector. Open a web countdown such as the date countdown calculator for a set start time, or the countdown timer for a fixed break, then make the browser full screen and zoom in. Turn off display sleep and notifications first.

Can I share an event countdown with friends?

In Left, Joint Ahead dates (part of Left Premium) let friends count down to the same date on their own phones; see how Joint Ahead dates work. With any app, sending a calendar invite makes sure everyone has the same start time.
