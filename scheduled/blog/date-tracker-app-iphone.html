The Best Date Tracker Apps for iPhone: Days Until and Days Since

Updated November 30, 2026 · 8 min read

The dates you care about point in two directions. Some are ahead of you: a trip in March, your sister's birthday, an exam, a contract that ends in June. Others are behind you: the day you quit smoking, the day you met your partner, the day you moved cities, the last time you changed the water filter. The first kind needs a countdown. The second needs a count-up.

Most date apps are built for one direction. Countdown apps are great at "34 days until Lisbon" and awkward at "212 days since my last cigarette". Days-since apps are the reverse. A good date tracker handles both, lets each date show the unit that makes sense, and puts the numbers on your Home Screen and Lock Screen so you don't have to open anything to see them.

Disclosure: we make Left, so we've put it first. Here's why, and when another app suits you better.

Dates ahead and dates behind

Sort your dates first. It tells you which kind of app you need.

Dates ahead (count down)

  • Events: a holiday, a concert, a wedding, a race, a move.
  • Deadlines: a tax return, a thesis hand-in, a visa expiry, a lease renewal.
  • People: birthdays and anniversaries coming up.
  • Milestones: the last day of term, a due date, a retirement date.

Dates behind (count up)

  • Things you quit: alcohol, nicotine, doomscrolling before bed.
  • Things you started: a new job, a sober year, living in a new city, owning a dog.
  • Relationships: days since you met, days together.
  • Maintenance: the last oil change, filter swap, haircut, or time you called your grandmother.

If almost everything on your list is ahead, a countdown app is enough. If almost everything is behind, a days-since app is enough. If you have a mix, which most people do, you want one app that does both, or you'll end up with two apps, two sets of widgets and two places to check.

One-off dates and repeating dates

A trip happens once. A birthday comes back every year, and rent comes back every month. A date tracker should let a repeating date roll forward by itself, so the day after the birthday it starts counting to the next one instead of showing "1 day ago".

Count-ups repeat differently. "Days since I changed the filter" doesn't recur on a schedule; it resets when you do the thing again. So for dates behind, look for a restart button, and ideally a history of past restarts so you can see that the filter usually lasts about 90 days.

Days, weeks or months: pick the unit per date

"412 days" is hard to feel. "13 months" is easy. "2 days" is clearer than "0 months". The right unit depends on the distance:

  • Hours: the last day before a flight or an exam.
  • Days: anything under a few months away, and most early quit streaks.
  • Weeks: pregnancies, training plans, semesters.
  • Months or years: relationships, long sobriety, time in a job, a retirement date.

The best apps let each date use its own unit, and let a list widget mix them. If you only need a quick answer, the days between two dates calculator and the days since a date calculator work in your browser without an app.

What makes a date tracker good

  • Both directions in one app, with the same widgets and look.
  • Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets, including one that lists several dates at once.
  • Repeats for birthdays and renewals, and restarts for count-ups.
  • A unit per date: hours, days, weeks, months or years.
  • Something for the last stretch, such as a reminder a week out or a live countdown on the Lock Screen on the day.
  • A place for context: notes or a packing list on a trip, the reason you quit on a streak.

2. Countdown Star

Countdown Star is a dedicated countdown app for iPhone, built around good-looking event cards and widgets.

Best for: people who mostly count down and care about how the countdown looks.

Pros: focused on one job; widgets are the point of the app.

Cons: count-ups aren't its focus, so dates behind you may need a second app.

3. Big Days

Big Days is an event countdown app that leans on photos: each date gets an image, and the widget shows it with the days left. It suits trips and celebrations, where seeing the place or the people helps.

Best for: holidays, weddings and birthdays you want to look at, not just read.

Pros: photo-first design; works well for exciting dates.

Cons: less suited to dry deadlines or count-ups from the past.

4. DayCount

DayCount counts both before and after a date, with a detailed widget builder for fonts, colors and backgrounds.

Best for: people who want both directions and like to style each widget.

Pros: counts both ways; lots of widget customization.

Cons: the styling options take time to set up.

5. Days Since

Days Since does the other half: it counts up from the last time something happened and lets you reset it. It's a simple fit for quit counters and maintenance reminders.

Best for: count-ups only, such as quitting something or tracking chores.

Pros: simple, single-purpose; quick to reset.

Cons: no countdowns, so dates ahead live somewhere else.

The baseline: Apple Calendar

Apple Calendar already knows many of your dates, including birthdays from Contacts, and its widget shows what's next. What it doesn't do is turn a date into a number. It won't tell you "23 days until" at a glance, and it has no idea how long it's been since you quit. In Left you can bring an event in with From calendar when you add an Ahead date.

Set it up in Left

A date ahead

  1. Open Left, go to Ahead and tap +. Type a name, pick a ready-made date, or choose From calendar.
  2. Set when it starts. For a trip, add when it ends too.
  3. For birthdays and anniversaries, set Repeat to yearly.
  4. Under More options, choose how to Show as... (hours, days, weeks, months or years), add a reminder, and turn on Get ready for the start.
  5. Open the saved date and add notes or todos: flight numbers, a packing list, gift ideas.

A date behind

  1. Go to Since, tap + and choose a streak.
  2. Name it ("No alcohol", "Changed water filter", "Together") and set Started on... to the real date, even if it was years ago.
  3. When it happens again, tap Restart. Your longest and average streak stay.

Put them on your screens

  1. Touch and hold the Home Screen, tap Edit, then Add Widget, and search for Left.
  2. Add an Ahead widget for one countdown, or the Time Ahead list for several. Add a Streak widget or the Streaks list for count-ups.
  3. On the Lock Screen, add the Ahead widget (circular or rectangular) or the rectangular Streak widget for the one or two dates you check most.
  4. On Apple Watch, edit your watch face and add the Ahead or Streak complication.

More detail on each side: the countdown widget page for dates ahead, and the days since widget page for dates behind.

Questions

What is a date tracker app?

A date tracker app keeps the dates that matter to you and shows how far away they are. Good ones count down to dates ahead, like trips and birthdays, and count up from dates behind, like the day you quit something or met someone.

Can one app count down and count up?

Yes. Left uses Ahead for countdowns and streaks for count-ups, and DayCount also counts both ways. Countdown-only and days-since-only apps handle just one direction.

How do I make a birthday countdown repeat every year?

Set the date to repeat yearly. In Left, set Repeat to yearly when you create or edit the Ahead date, and it rolls on to the next birthday by itself.

Can I count up from a date in the past?

Yes. In Left, create a streak and set Started on... to the real date, even if it was years ago. The count starts from that date, not from the day you added it.

Can I show a date in weeks or months instead of days?

Yes, in apps that let you choose a unit. In Left, each date can show hours, days, weeks, months or years, and the list widgets let each date use its own unit.

Does iPhone have a built-in date tracker?

Not as such. Apple Calendar stores your events and birthdays and shows what's next, but it doesn't show days until or days since as a number. For that you need a date tracker app or a quick calculator.
