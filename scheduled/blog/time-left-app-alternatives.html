6 Best Time Left App Alternatives for iPhone in 2026

Updated November 29, 2026 · 8 min read

Checked 21 October 2026. Every competitor detail below comes from that app's US App Store page and Apple's public lookup data, fetched that day. Left details come from the app itself.

Time Left • Be Present by Julien Lacroix has been on the US App Store since 2020 and has 4.6 stars from 240 ratings. It shows the days, months and years you have left, a Life in Weeks calendar, and moments you add yourself, with no ads, no notifications and no sign-up. People look for an alternative when they want widgets without a Pro purchase, a different way to see their lifespan, a journal, or a longer track record. This category is small: most of the alternatives below have fewer than 40 ratings, so we say so wherever it matters.

Disclosure: we make Left, so we have put it first. Here is why, and when another app suits you better. Left also has a view called Time Left, which is not the same thing as the app named Time Left.

Quick verdict

  • Best alternative if you want your life, year and countdowns together, with free widgets: Left.
  • Best free dots grid with no in-app purchases: Life Dots.
  • Best for a memento mori feel, with a journal: Memento Mori: Death Clock.
  • Best for a weekly ritual and an automatic wallpaper: WeeksAlive.
  • Best for a 4,000-weeks grid with a short journal: Life Time Left: 4K Weeks.
  • Best for one purchase and notes and photos on each week: Life Calendar: Visual Timeline.

What Time Left does well

Its App Store page says it shows "the days, months, and years you have left". The developer says he designed it without any external framework, so your birthdate stays private, and the App Store privacy label says Data Not Linked to You (purchases). You can add moments, which are countdowns or memories with your own photos, and the latest update improved the Life in Weeks layout for iPhone Duo. It runs on iPhone and Apple Vision.

  • Price: free to download. Time Left Pro unlocks widgets and skins, unlimited moments and Live Photos in the Life Calendar. Its in-app purchases include weekly and yearly subscriptions and one-time and lifetime purchases.
  • Updates: version 2026.23 came out on 12 October 2026, and the app has been on the App Store since 2020.

If you want a calm, private life-in-weeks calendar and don't need widgets or a long list of extras, Time Left is hard to beat. The alternatives below make sense when your needs are different.

1. Left: your life, your year and the dates ahead

Left is a time awareness app for iPhone. Its Time Left section shows your life, year, month, week, today and the current hour as grids of symbols, where the past dims and what remains stays solid. Countdowns live in Ahead and habits and streaks in Since.

  • Your life: enter your birthday and Left shows your life, with your name if you set one. In Settings you can see your life in Years, Months or Weeks, or hide the Life view.
  • Lifespan: the default is 80 years. Calculating a lifespan from factors such as exercise, smoking and sleep, or setting one manually, is part of Left Premium. It is an estimate for reflection, not medical advice.
  • Widgets: the Life widget (small, medium and large, plus Lock Screen) shows one symbol per year of your lifespan, with years left or the percentage left. The You widget shows time left and lived. There are also Year, Month, Week and Day widgets, and these widgets are free.
  • Wallpaper: Left Wallpaper can show your life, year or month on the Lock Screen, and it's free. Setting it to update itself every day takes a short Shortcuts automation, which the app walks you through.
  • Moments: Ahead dates cover the countdown side of Time Left's moments, with a photo, a symbol, reminders and a Live Activity for the final countdown. Left has no place for past memories.

Best for: people who want the life view alongside the year, the month and the dates they're looking forward to, with widgets and a wallpaper on the Lock Screen.

Price: free to download. The free plan includes the Time Left views, the widgets, the wallpaper and 2 countdowns, 2 habits, 2 streaks and 2 Aims, and the count includes every item you've ever created, so deleting one doesn't free a slot. Left Premium is a subscription or a lifetime purchase. Prices are shown in the app before you buy.

Pros: the life, year and month views are free; free widgets on the Home Screen and Lock Screen; a wallpaper; countdowns, habits and goals in the same app.

Cons: no journal; a custom lifespan needs Left Premium; iPhone only, and it needs iOS 26 or later, while Time Left runs on older iPhones and Apple Vision; no Apple Watch app.

2. Life Dots: Visualize Time

By THE DIGITAL MINIMALIST PTE. LTD. Its App Store page says "See how much of your day, month, year, or expected lifespan has passed through a simple grid of dots", with a widget for the Home Screen or Lock Screen. It lists iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Vision in its compatibility.

Best for: people who want a free, plain dots grid for the day, month, year and lifespan.

Price: free to download, with no in-app purchases listed.

Pros: free; 5.0 from 34 ratings; updated in September 2026; privacy label says Data Not Collected.

Cons: the listing doesn't say how the lifespan is set or name a weeks view; it has few ratings; no moments or countdowns are described.

3. Memento Mori: Death Clock

By Ramkumar Vellaisamy. Its App Store page lists a Life Countdown in years, months, days, hours or seconds, Life Blocks, described as a "visual grid showing your life progress at a glance", Home Screen widgets and a customizable life expectancy. Its release notes mention a Life Journal. It runs on iPhone and iPad.

Best for: people who want the memento mori theme, a countdown in several units and a journal.

Price: free to download. Its in-app purchases are a Pro Lifetime item and support-style tiers such as A Day of Impact and A Week of Support, and the listing doesn't say what they unlock.

Pros: an adjustable life expectancy; 4.9 from 21 ratings; Data Not Linked to You on its privacy label.

Cons: the listing doesn't name a weeks grid or Lock Screen widgets; what the paid tiers include isn't clear; few ratings.

4. Life in Weeks - WeeksAlive

By Adrien Cens. Its App Store page says "Every dot is a week you lived, or a week still ahead", and adds a daily 60-second ritual with four questions, one to three weekly intentions with a weekly recap, and widgets and an automatic wallpaper for the Home Screen and Lock Screen.

Best for: people who want a life calendar with a daily check-in and a wallpaper, not just a grid.

Price: free to download with a free trial. The listing says WeeksAlive Pro is an optional auto-renewing subscription and that a purchase is required to use Pro features after the trial, without saying which features are Pro.

Pros: a weeks grid with widgets and a wallpaper; updated in October 2026.

Cons: only 5 ratings; the privacy label lists identifiers and usage data as used to track you, so read it before you install; which features need Pro isn't stated.

5. Life Time Left: 4K Weeks

By Edwing H Santos Mena. Its App Store page describes a Life Calendar of about 4,000 weeks that shows the weeks spent, the weeks left and the percentage left, plus a Stoic Journal for short thoughts with a mood tag and a history view. It says your data stays on your device and iCloud.

Best for: people drawn to the 4,000-weeks idea who want a short journal next to the grid.

Price: free to download. The listing says core features require a subscription, and a lifetime purchase is also listed.

Pros: a clean weeks grid; a lifetime option; 4.6 from 18 ratings.

Cons: core features are paid; widgets appear only in its release notes and no wallpaper is mentioned; last updated in February 2026.

6. Life Calendar: Visual Timeline

By Bence Csondor. Its App Store page describes one square for every week you have lived, with notes and photos you can attach to a week, and memories and milestones. Widgets appear only in its release notes: version 1.2.0 added Smart Widgets for "Memories on your Home Screen".

Best for: people who want to pay once and keep notes and photos on each week of their life.

Price: free to download, with a one-time Full Access (Lifetime) purchase. The listing doesn't say what the free version includes.

Pros: a one-time purchase instead of a subscription; notes and photos per week.

Cons: 3.7 from 12 ratings; a short description that gives no widget or lifespan details; last updated in May 2026.

How we chose

  • Starting point: US App Store searches for "life in weeks", "life calendar", "memento mori" and "death clock" on 20 and 21 October 2026, plus the apps listed as alternatives to Time Left on comparison sites.
  • Kept: five apps that show your life as a grid or a countdown and were updated in the last 12 months.
  • Left out: longevity and health apps such as Death Clock: The Life Lab, entertainment quizzes that predict a death date, and apps not updated for a year. We also left out apps with no ratings yet.
  • Sources: each app's own App Store page and Apple's lookup data. No paid placements, and no developer reviewed this page.
  • Fair cons: where a listing doesn't mention a feature we say so instead of assuming it's missing. Most apps here have very few ratings, so treat the star scores lightly. Check the App Store page before you buy, because plans change.

Questions

Is the Time Left app free?

It is free to download on the US App Store. Its listing says Time Left Pro unlocks widgets and skins, unlimited moments and Live Photos in the Life Calendar. Its in-app purchases include weekly and yearly subscriptions and one-time and lifetime purchases (checked 21 October 2026).

What is the best free alternative to Time Left?

Life Dots lists no in-app purchases and shows a dots grid for your day, month, year or expected lifespan, with a Home Screen or Lock Screen widget. Left is free to download, shows your life in years, months or weeks, and its life, year and countdown widgets are free.

Does Time Left have widgets?

Its App Store page lists widgets as part of Time Left Pro. The developer's site describes life, countdown and year progress widgets. Left, Life Dots, Memento Mori: Death Clock and WeeksAlive name widgets in their descriptions. Life Time Left: 4K Weeks and Life Calendar: Visual Timeline mention widgets only in their release notes.

Which alternative shows my life in weeks?

Left lets you see your life in years, months or weeks. WeeksAlive, Life Time Left: 4K Weeks and Life Calendar: Visual Timeline each describe a grid with a square or dot for every week.

Can I change the life expectancy these apps use?

Memento Mori: Death Clock lists a customizable life expectancy, Time Left's website says its views are based on your chosen life expectancy, and Life Time Left: 4K Weeks asks for your life expectancy during setup. In Left, the default lifespan is 80 years, and setting your own lifespan is part of Left Premium. Left's lifespan is an estimate for reflection, not medical advice.
