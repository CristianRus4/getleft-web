Time Management Widgets for iPhone: Which Ones Help and How to Set Them Up

Updated December 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Most time management advice assumes you'll open a planner and look at it. The problem is that you won't, at least not at 2:40 pm when you've lost an hour to a rabbit hole. What you will do is unlock your phone, about a hundred times a day. A widget turns those unlocks into small check-ins: how much of the day is left, what's next, and whether you're doing what you meant to.

This guide sorts time management widgets for iPhone into three jobs, shows which built-in and third-party widgets do each one, and gives three Home Screen layouts you can copy.

The three jobs a time management widget can do

A widget that tries to do everything ends up showing nothing clearly. Think of each widget as doing one of three jobs:

  1. Awareness: how much time is left. Of today, this week, this month, this year. This is the job most setups skip, and the one that does the most against "where did the afternoon go?"
  2. Commitments: what's coming. The next meeting, today's tasks, the deadline in 9 days.
  3. Execution: what you're doing now. A focus timer, today's habits, the one thing you're working on.

A good Home Screen has one widget for each job, not five for the same one.

Awareness widgets: see time passing

A clock tells you what time it is. It doesn't tell you that 70% of your working day is gone. Progress widgets do, and that small shift changes decisions: "it's 3 pm" is neutral; "two hours of work left" makes you pick what matters.

  • Day progress: how much of today has passed, shown as a grid, ring or bar. Left's Time Left widgets show this for the day, the hour, the week, the month and the year. The day and hour grids are especially good for work blocks.
  • Week and month progress: useful on Monday mornings and in the last week of a month, when "plenty of time" quietly runs out.
  • Year progress: the long view. A year progress widget showing 86% done in mid-November is a better prompt than any resolution list.

Commitment widgets: see what's next

  • Calendar (built in): Apple's Calendar widget shows your next events. The medium size is the sweet spot: enough to see the next two or three, small enough to leave room.
  • Reminders (built in): shows a list, and you can tick items off from the widget.
  • Countdowns: for deadlines measured in days or weeks, a countdown beats a calendar entry because it shows the gap, not the date. "Report due in 9 days" lands differently from "Nov 18". Left's Ahead widgets do this; see the countdown widget page.
  • Planner views: some apps combine events, reminders and habits for today in one widget. Left's Planner (part of Left Premium) does this, and can bring in Todoist tasks.

Execution widgets: stay on the task

  • Focus timer: a Pomodoro-style timer you can start from the Home Screen. Left's Focus widget (Focus is part of Left Premium) has a Start button and a Pause or Resume button, shows the current phase and countdown, and a strip of today's sessions. The Pomodoro timer page shows how it works.
  • Today's habits: the routines that make the day work (a walk at lunch, a hard stop at 6). Left's Habits list widget shows several at once.
  • Clock timer (built in): fine for one-off timers, but it lives in the Clock app; it isn't a Home Screen tool.

How to add and arrange the widgets

  1. Touch and hold an empty spot on the Home Screen until the icons jiggle, then tap Edit and Add Widget.
  2. Search for the app (Calendar, Left), pick a widget and a size, and tap Add Widget.
  3. Touch and hold the widget and tap Edit Widget to choose what it shows: which countdown, which habit, which time grid.
  4. Stack widgets of the same size by dragging one onto another, if you want to swipe between them.

Rules that keep it useful

  • First page only. Time widgets on page three don't get seen.
  • One widget per job. Two calendars and three timers is noise.
  • Avoid Smart Stack rotation for the important one. If iOS rotates it away, it's not there when you need it.
  • Change it when you stop seeing it. After a few weeks your eye skips familiar widgets. Swap the size or position and it registers again.

Questions

What is a time management widget?

A Home Screen or Lock Screen widget that helps you manage time without opening an app: showing how much of the day is left, what's coming next, or a timer for the task you're on.

Does iPhone have built-in time management widgets?

Yes, partly. Calendar, Reminders and Clock all have widgets for upcoming events, lists and world time. iPhone doesn't have a built-in widget for how much of the day, week or year has passed; apps like Left add those.

Can I start a focus timer from a widget?

Yes, with an app that has an interactive timer widget. Left's Focus widget has a Start button and a Pause or Resume button that work from the Home Screen without opening the app. Focus is part of Left Premium.

How many widgets should I use for time management?

Usually three: one for time left, one for what's next, and one for what you're doing now. More than that tends to become background noise.

Can I put a time management widget on the Lock Screen?

Yes. iPhone supports small circular and rectangular Lock Screen widgets. A countdown or a focus timer works well there because you see it every time you pick up the phone.
