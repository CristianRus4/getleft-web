Holiday Countdown App for iPhone: Every Holiday in One Widget (2027)

Updated November 9, 2026 · 8 min read

Most people look for a holiday countdown app on iPhone in late November, add a Christmas countdown, and delete it on December 26. Then Lunar New Year, Easter and Halloween each need another app. There's a better way: set up every holiday once, in one app, and let one widget show what's coming next.

Below: which holidays can repeat on their own, which ones move every year, the widget that shows several at once, and December extras for the big one. The examples use Left, the app we make, but the rules apply to any countdown app.

What a holiday countdown app on iPhone should do

An app you keep all year needs a few things a Christmas-only app doesn't:

  • Several dates in one widget. Six holidays in six widgets takes up a whole Home Screen page.
  • Yearly repeat. Christmas and Halloween should come back on their own, without you re-adding them.
  • A unit per date. "46 days" to Christmas is useful. "139 days" to Easter is noise; "about 20 weeks" reads better.
  • Lock Screen and StandBy. In December, that is where you look most.
  • An honest answer for moving holidays. No yearly repeat setting knows that Easter 2027 falls on March 28. Someone has to check.
  • Sharing, if counting down with family is part of the fun.

Fixed holidays vs. holidays that move

Some holidays sit on the same calendar date every year: New Year's Day (January 1), Valentine's Day (February 14), Independence Day in the US (July 4), Halloween (October 31) and Christmas (December 25). A yearly repeat handles these perfectly.

Many others follow a weekday rule or a lunar calendar, so they land on a different date each year. A yearly repeat brings a countdown back on the same day and month, which means a repeated Easter countdown would be wrong by next spring. These are the big ones that move:

HolidayHow the date is setUsual windowNext date
Thanksgiving (US)Fourth Thursday of NovemberNov 22 to 28Nov 26, 2026
Hanukkah25 Kislev in the Hebrew calendar, starting at sundownLate Nov to late DecEvening of Dec 4, 2026
Lunar New YearA new moon in the Chinese lunisolar calendarJan 21 to Feb 20Feb 6, 2027
Eid al-FitrEnd of Ramadan in the Islamic lunar calendarAbout 11 days earlier each yearAround Mar 9 or 10, 2027
Easter (Western)The Sunday after the church's spring full moonMar 22 to Apr 25Mar 28, 2027
DiwaliThe new moon of the Hindu month of KartikaMid-Oct to mid-NovOct 29, 2027

Eid depends on the moon sighting and can differ by a day between countries; Diwali and Lunar New Year can also shift by a day depending on where you live. Check with your local community, mosque, temple or synagogue before you lock a date in. Weekday-rule holidays move too: in the US, Mother's Day is the second Sunday in May and Memorial Day is the last Monday in May.

So the rule is simple: repeat the fixed holidays, check the moving ones once a year. For a quick look without any setup, there are live pages for days until Easter and days until Thanksgiving.

Set up the whole year once

Here is the setup in Left. You do it once.

Fixed holidays: add once, repeat yearly

  1. Open Left and go to Ahead. Tap Add a date ahead (or +).
  2. Search the ready-made dates for the holiday. If it isn't there, type a name or choose New date.
  3. Set Starts to the day it begins. For something that lasts, like a week off over Christmas, set Until to the last day.
  4. Set Repeat to yearly, then save.

Moving holidays: add this year's date, then update it

Add Easter, Thanksgiving, Lunar New Year, Diwali, Eid or Hanukkah the same way, but check the date against the table above or your own calendar, and leave Repeat off. The day after the holiday, look up next year's date and change Starts. It takes 30 seconds, and the countdown is right for another year.

If the holiday already shows in Apple's Calendar app (it can show a holiday calendar for your region), choose From calendar instead to import its date and time.

Use the start time, not just the date

Hanukkah begins at sundown, so set Starts to the evening of December 4 rather than midnight. For New Year, set Starts to 12:00 AM on January 1. The countdown then ends at the moment the holiday actually begins.

One widget for every holiday

Left's Time Ahead list widget shows up to 7 dates in the Large size (3 in Medium). To add it, touch and hold an empty part of your Home Screen, tap Edit, then Add Widget, search for Left and choose Time Ahead list.

Each date in the list keeps its own unit, which you set in the app under More options → Show as… (hours, days, weeks, months or years). A simple scheme:

Give each holiday its own look

Tap the preview card at the top of a date and choose Add image to pick a photo from your library or Unsplash, or a symbol. The single-date Ahead widget can show that photo behind the count: last year's tree for Christmas, lanterns for Lunar New Year, a pumpkin for Halloween.

December: put the big one on the Lock Screen

For the holiday that matters most to you, go beyond the Home Screen:

  • Lock Screen widget. Touch and hold the Lock Screen, tap Customize, choose Lock Screen, tap the widget area and add Left's Ahead widget in the circular or rectangular size, then tap it to pick the holiday.
  • Live Activity for the final hours. In the date's options, turn on Get ready for the start. In the hours before the holiday begins, a countdown shows on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. With New Year set to midnight, that covers the last hours of December 31.
  • StandBy. Left widgets work in StandBy, so a phone charging on its side on the nightstand can show the count too.
  • A wallpaper that counts. Left Wallpaper is free and updates itself through the Shortcuts app. Its Special Date mode counts down to one of your Ahead dates. See how to set up the wallpaper shortcut.

Without a countdown app

Apple's Calendar app can show your region's holidays, and its widget lists what's coming up. It doesn't count the days for you, though.

Other countdown apps can cover parts of this setup. If you're comparing another holiday countdown timer app, check three things: whether a widget can show more than one date, whether dates can repeat yearly, and what happens to Easter and the other moving holidays next year.

Questions

What is the best holiday countdown app for iPhone?

Look for one that shows several holidays in one widget, repeats fixed dates yearly, lets you pick days or weeks per date, and works on the Lock Screen. Left does all four; many countdown apps cover some of them.

Can a holiday countdown repeat every year?

Yes, for holidays on a fixed date such as Christmas, New Year and Halloween: set Repeat to yearly. Easter, Thanksgiving, Lunar New Year, Diwali, Eid and Hanukkah fall on a different date each year, so update those countdowns once a year instead.

How do I check the days until New Year or Halloween without an app?
