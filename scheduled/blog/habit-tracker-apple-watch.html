The Best Habit Tracker Apps for Apple Watch in 2026

Updated November 9, 2026 · 8 min read

The best moment to log a habit is right after you do it: the glass of water is still in your hand, the run has just ended, the book is closing. That's also the moment your iPhone is usually in another room. A habit tracker on Apple Watch closes that gap. If logging takes one tap on your wrist, you log it. If it takes finding your phone, unlocking it and opening an app, you tell yourself you'll do it later, and later is how habit data quietly goes missing.

Apple Watch doesn't come with a habit tracker. Activity rings cover movement, Mindfulness covers breathing, and Reminders can nag you, but none of them counts "read 10 pages" or "no coffee after 2 pm" day after day. For that you need an app. Here are the habit trackers that work well on Apple Watch, what each one is built for, and how to set one up so your wrist does most of the work.

Disclosure: we make Left, so we've put it first. Here's why, and when another app suits you better.

What makes a habit tracker good on Apple Watch

Plenty of iPhone habit apps ship a Watch app that is little more than a list. On the wrist, four things matter more than anything on the feature list:

  • One-tap logging. Raise, tap, done. If the Watch app asks you to scroll through menus or confirm, you'll stop using it.
  • A complication. A habit you can see on the watch face gets done more often than one hidden inside an app. The best trackers let you put today's progress, or one specific habit, right on the face.
  • Undo. Wrist taps are clumsy. You need a quick way to take back an accidental check-in without picking up the phone.
  • Reliable sync. A habit logged on the watch has to show up on the iPhone, in widgets and in your streak. If it doesn't, you'll stop trusting both.

Beyond that, think about which habits you'd actually log from the watch. Physical ones (water, stretching, walks, pills) suit it best. Habits you do at a desk can just as easily be logged on the phone.

1. Left: habits, streaks and Aims on your wrist

Left is a time awareness app for iPhone with an Apple Watch app. Habits live in Since alongside two other trackers: streaks, which count up on their own from a start date (good for quitting something), and Aims, which count a number up or down (books read, kilometers run, money saved). All three are on the watch.

  • Habits on the watch: see what's due today, grouped by status, and log a habit with one tap. Undo a completion if you tapped by mistake. Each habit shows the last 7 days, the streak and habit strength.
  • Streaks on the watch: check the current streak, restarts, average and longest streak, reset a streak when it breaks, and undo a reset made today.
  • Aims on the watch: log progress toward a number goal (add 2 km, subtract 1 kg) and see the target, deadline and progress bar.
  • Complications: Habit, Habits Today, Streak and Aim complications in circular, rectangular, inline and corner sizes, next to Year, Month, Week, Day and Life progress and Ahead countdowns. Habit, Streak and Aim complications can each show a specific item.
  • On the iPhone: flexible schedules (every day, weekdays, specific days, a number of days per week, or weekly and monthly targets), up to 4 reminders per habit, Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets, and a persistent reminder that shows a Live Activity an hour before a time you pick if the habit still isn't logged.

You choose which screens appear on the watch from the iPhone app, so the watch can show only Habits and Streaks if that's all you use.

Best for: people who want habits, quit streaks and number goals in one place, with complications for each, and who like seeing how much of the day, week and year is left.

Price: Left is free to download. The free plan includes 2 habits and 2 streaks (plus 2 Aims and 2 countdowns), counted over everything you've ever created. Left Premium is a subscription or lifetime purchase. Prices are shown in the app before you buy.

Pros: three kinds of tracker on the watch, not just checkboxes; complications for a single habit or for today's list; undo for habits and streak resets; habit strength softens the blow of one missed day.

2. Streaks

Streaks is one of the best-known habit apps on iOS and has had an Apple Watch app for years. It keeps your list deliberately short, and habits linked to the Health app can complete themselves, so a step or workout goal ticks off without you touching anything. Negative habits ("don't bite nails") work too.

Best for: a focused daily checklist on iPhone and Apple Watch, paid once.

Pros: polished Watch app; Health auto-completion; no subscription.

Cons: a hard cap on the number of tasks; no free version; built around daily check-ins rather than number goals. See Streaks alternatives if it's not quite right.

3. Habitify

Habitify is a cross-platform habit tracker with a Watch app, detailed statistics and habits organized by time of day. It suits people who like to review charts and completion rates as much as they like ticking boxes.

Best for: data-minded people who also use the tracker on other devices.

Pros: rich stats; morning, afternoon and evening grouping; available beyond Apple's platforms.

Cons: the best features sit behind a subscription; more app than you need for a handful of habits. More options in Habitify alternatives.

4. Productive

Productive builds habits into routines for different parts of the day, with reminders and a Watch app to check things off. It leans toward planning your day around habits rather than simply counting them.

Best for: people who want a morning and evening routine, not just a list.

Pros: time-of-day routines; motivating design; Watch app.

Cons: subscription-led; a lot of structure if you only want to track two or three things.

5. Strides

Strides tracks habits alongside targets, averages and projects, so "floss daily" and "save a set amount by June" can live in the same app. It has an Apple Watch app for logging.

Best for: people who mix yes-or-no habits with numeric goals.

Pros: several tracker types; pace lines show whether a goal is on track.

Cons: the flexibility takes longer to set up; advanced features are paid.

6. Days Since

Days Since isn't a daily check-in app. It counts how long it has been since you last did something you're trying to stop, and shows that count on iPhone and Apple Watch.

Best for: quit counters rather than daily habits.

Pros: simple, single-purpose; Watch app.

Cons: no daily checklist, so you'll need a second app for habits you build rather than break.

How to set up a habit tracker on Apple Watch (with Left)

  1. Install Left on your iPhone. The Watch app installs with it if automatic app install is on; otherwise open the Watch app on your iPhone and install Left from the list.
  2. In Left on iPhone, open Since and tap +. Type the habit ("Drink water", "Stretch") to pick a template, or choose Build from scratch.
  3. Set the period and how many times. "Drink water, 6 times a day" fills up with each tap on the watch.
  4. Turn on Get reminders for the times you usually do it, and turn on Get a persistent reminder for habits you tend to forget late in the day.
  5. On the watch, open Left and swipe to Habits. Tap a habit to log it. Tap again to undo.
  6. Long-press your watch face, tap Edit, and add the Habits Today complication, or a Habit complication for the one habit you most want to see.

Want the same habit on your phone too? Add the Habit widget to your Home Screen or Lock Screen; the habit tracker widget page walks through it.

What to track from your wrist, and what to leave on the phone

  • Great on the watch: water, vitamins or medication, stretching, short walks, posture breaks, "no snooze". Anything physical, frequent or done away from your desk.
  • Fine on either: reading, journaling, practicing an instrument. Log wherever you are when you finish.
  • Better as a streak than a habit: things you're quitting. "No alcohol" is easier as a counter that runs by itself until you reset it, which is what Left's streaks do. If you're unsure which to use, read streaks vs habits.
  • Better as an Aim: anything with a number. "Run 100 km this quarter" is an Aim you add to after each run, not a daily checkbox.

Keep the watch list short. Three to five habits on the wrist is plenty; more than that and the Habits screen becomes a scroll, which defeats the point.

Questions

Does Apple Watch have a built-in habit tracker?

No. Activity rings track movement and the Mindfulness app tracks breathing, but there's no built-in way to track habits like reading or drinking water. You need a third-party app with a Watch app, such as Left, Streaks or Habitify.

Does a habit logged on the watch show up on my iPhone?

Yes. Left's Watch app syncs with the iPhone app when you open it and periodically while you use it, and keeps recent data cached so the watch still shows your habits when the phone isn't immediately reachable.

Can I show a habit on my watch face?

Yes, with an app that has complications. Left has a Habit complication for one habit and a Habits Today complication for today's list, in circular, rectangular, inline and corner sizes.

What's the best free habit tracker for Apple Watch?

Left is free to download and its free plan includes 2 habits and 2 streaks that you can log from the watch. If you need many habits for free, compare the free limits of each app before you commit, since most put unlimited habits behind a paid plan.

Should I track quitting habits as habits or streaks?

Usually as streaks. A streak counts up on its own from the day you stopped and only changes when you reset it, so there's nothing to tick off each day. In Left you can reset a streak from the watch and undo the reset if it was a mistake.
