Study Countdown Timer on iPhone: Plan Every Session to Exam Day (2027)

Updated December 8, 2026 · 7 min read

A study countdown timer is really two timers. One counts down the days to your exam so you can plan revision. The other counts down the minutes of the study session in front of you. This guide shows how to connect them on your iPhone: turn the days left into a number of study sessions, spread those sessions across the weeks, then run each one with a timer.

All you need is your iPhone's Clock app and a sheet of paper. There's also a section on doing it all in Left.

An exam countdown alone tends to cause panic. A session timer alone keeps you busy without telling you whether you're on track. Together, they tell you how many sessions you have left and what each one is for.

Work out how many study sessions you have

Here's the whole method in one line:

study days left × sessions per day = sessions left

A worked example. Today is Monday, November 9. Your chemistry final is on Monday, December 14. That's 35 days, or exactly 5 weeks.

  1. Count your study days. You study on weekdays only: 5 weeks × 5 days = 25 days.
  2. Take out the days you won't study. Thanksgiving week costs you 2 days, and you keep Friday, December 11 free to rest before the exam. That leaves 25 − 3 = 22 study days.
  3. Pick a realistic daily number. Three 25-minute sessions on a school day is doable for most students, with room for homework in your other classes.
  4. Multiply. 22 × 3 = 66 sessions, or about 27.5 hours of focused study.

66 is a number you can plan with. With 6 topics plus past papers, each topic gets roughly 6 to 8 sessions, with some left for practice. If the total is too small, you find out in week 1, not week 5.

Several exams? Run the sum for each, and give the earliest one more of the early sessions.

Plan the sessions by week

A week is the right unit for a study plan: a daily plan breaks the first time you get sick, and a term plan is too vague to act on. Here are the 66 sessions from the example:

WeekSessionsWhat they're for
1 (Nov 9–15)15Topics 1 and 2: notes, then questions on each
2 (Nov 16–22)15Topics 3 and 4
3 (Nov 23–29)9Topic 5, then a quick review of 1 and 2 (short week)
4 (Nov 30–Dec 6)15Topic 6, then mixed practice questions on all topics
5 (Dec 7–13)12Past papers in timed conditions, then go over every mistake

Three rules keep a plan like this honest:

  • Give every session a job before it starts. "Topic 3: 10 practice questions" beats "chemistry." Write the jobs for the week on Sunday.
  • Start practice before the last week. Testing yourself shows what you don't know while there's still time to fix it.
  • Keep a buffer. Plan for a few sessions fewer than your total. You will lose some.

Run each session with a study timer on iPhone

The 25/5 rhythm (25 minutes on, 5 off, a longer break after four rounds) is the usual starting point because it's short enough to begin when you don't feel like it. Match the length to the task:

  • 25/5: flashcards, reading, short review, and days when starting is the hard part.
  • 45/10 or 50/10: problem sets, essays and anything where 25 minutes ends just as you get going. Count one long block as two sessions in your plan.
  • The real exam length: past papers. Time the whole paper in one go. A 2-hour paper counts as about four sessions.

The free way is the Clock app: tap Timers, set 25 minutes, tap Start. It shows on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island while it runs. You restart it for each break, and it keeps no record, so tally your sessions on paper next to your weekly plan. To keep messages away, turn on Do Not Disturb or a study Focus mode in Settings → Focus.

When the plan slips

It will. Don't fix it with an 8-hour Saturday. Redo the sum instead:

  1. Count the study days left and multiply by your daily sessions.
  2. List what's left to cover and give each item a number of sessions.
  3. If the second number is bigger, cut from the bottom: merge reviews, drop the lowest-weight topic to practice questions only, or add one session a day for a week.

Questions

What is a good study timer length?

Start with 25 minutes of study and a 5-minute break, with a longer break after four rounds. For problem sets or essays, try 45 or 50 minutes with a 10-minute break. For past papers, time the full exam length.

How do I count the days until my exam?

Enter the exam date in a date countdown calculator, or add it to a countdown app so the number updates every day. Then multiply the study days left by the sessions you can do per day to see how many study sessions you have.

Does the iPhone have a built-in study timer?

Not a dedicated one, but the Clock app works. Open Clock, tap Timers, set 25 minutes and tap Start. The timer shows on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. You restart it yourself for each break, and it doesn't keep a record of your sessions.

Can I put an exam countdown on my iPhone Lock Screen?

Yes, with a countdown widget app. In Left, add the exam in Ahead, then add the Ahead widget to your Lock Screen in the circular or rectangular size. To compare apps, see our list of the best exam countdown apps for iPhone.

How many study sessions do I need before an exam?

There's no single number. It depends on the course and how much you already know. Work backwards: study days left times sessions per day gives your total, then give each topic a share and keep some for practice questions and past papers. Check your syllabus or ask your teacher what the exam covers.
