# How to make Left widgets transparent

Left is designed to live outside the app as much as inside it. The Home Screen, Lock Screen, and StandBy widgets let you keep your time left, upcoming dates, habits, and streaks visible without opening Left every time you want to check them.

On iPhone, widget transparency is controlled by iOS, not by an individual setting inside Left. That means you first add the Left widget like any other iPhone widget, then use Apple's Home Screen customization controls to change the widget style. The `Clear` style makes supported widgets blend more naturally into your wallpaper, which is useful if you want a cleaner Home Screen or if you are building a setup where the wallpaper and widgets feel like one layout.

This guide shows the full flow: add the widget, enter Home Screen editing mode, then switch the widget appearance to `Clear`.

- [Step 2 - Enter jiggle mode](#step-2---enter-jiggle-mode)
- [Step 3 - Change widgets to Clear](#step-3---change-widgets-to-clear)
- [Troubleshooting](#troubleshooting)

## Step 1 - Add a Left widget

Start by adding the Left widget you want to use.

1. Long press an empty area of your iPhone Home Screen.
2. Tap the `+` button.
3. Search for `Left`.
4. Choose the widget you want and add it to your Home Screen.
5. Place it where you want it to appear.

The transparent style is controlled by iOS, so the widget needs to be on the Home Screen before you customize its appearance.

## Step 2 - Enter jiggle mode

Once the widget is on your Home Screen:

1. Long press the Home Screen again.
2. Wait until the apps and widgets start moving.
3. Stay on this screen. This is the same mode you use to move or remove apps and widgets.

## Step 3 - Change widgets to Clear

At the top of the screen:

1. Tap `Edit`.
2. Tap `Customize`.
3. Choose `Clear`.
4. Tap outside the customization panel or finish editing when you are done.

Your Left widgets should now use the clear Home Screen style, so they sit more naturally over your wallpaper.

## Troubleshooting

- I do not see `Clear`. Make sure your iPhone is updated to a version of iOS that supports clear widget styling.
- The widget still has a background. Remove the widget and add it again, then repeat `Edit` -> `Customize` -> `Clear`.
- Only some widgets changed. The clear option is an iOS Home Screen appearance setting. Check that you are customizing the correct Home Screen page and widget style.

# Streaks vs Habits

`Since` is the part of Left for tracking time that has already started. It is where you keep the streaks you want to protect, the habits you want to build, and the counters that remind you how long something meaningful has been part of your life.

That is why streaks and habits live together in the same tool. Both are about consistency over time, but they work differently. A streak is automatic: it keeps counting until you restart or reset it. A habit is action-based: you need to complete it on the schedule you chose, otherwise the habit can be missed and the habit streak can break.

The difference matters because choosing the wrong type makes the counter feel awkward. If something should keep going without you tapping every day, it should probably be a streak. If something requires repeated action from you, it should probably be a habit. Left gives both types templates, widgets, sharing, and detail views, but habit-specific features such as completion tracking, habit strength, and last chance reminders only make sense for habits.

## What is a streak?

A streak tracks time since a starting point or since the last reset. It keeps increasing automatically until you reset it.

Use a streak for something that does not need a daily tap from you. For example:

1. Days without alcohol.
2. Days since you started a relationship.
3. Days since you moved to a new city.
4. Days since you began a personal challenge.

The key idea is that the counter keeps running by itself. If the streak ends, you restart or reset the counter.

## What is a habit?

A habit tracks repeated actions that you need to complete. You choose how often the habit should happen, then mark it complete when you do it.

Habits can be daily, weekly, monthly, or scheduled for specific days. Some habits can also require more than one completion in the same period. For example:

1. Drink water 4 times per day.
2. Go to the gym 3 days per week.
3. Read every weekday.
4. Review finances once per month.

If you do not complete the habit when it is due, the habit streak can break.

## How they live together in Since

Open `Since` and tap the `+` button. You can choose either `Add a streak` or `Add an habit`.

Both streaks and habits appear in the same `Since` area because both are about time and consistency. You can also filter the view by `All counters`, `Streaks`, `Habits`, `Today`, or `Friends`, depending on what you want to focus on.

Templates are available for both types, so you do not always need to start from a blank item. Streak templates are useful for meaningful life counters. Habit templates are useful for routines with schedules, reminders, and completion goals.

## When to use each one

Choose a streak when the thing should continue automatically unless you reset it. A streak is best for "how long since..." tracking.

Choose a habit when you need to take action and mark it complete. A habit is best for routines where Left should know whether you did the work today, this week, or this month.

For example, "days without alcohol" works well as a streak because it keeps counting until you restart it. "Go for a run" works better as a habit because you need to complete it on the days you planned.

## Habit-only features

Some features are specific to habits because habits have completion data:

1. Habit strength insights show how consistent the habit has become.
2. Habit schedules control when the habit is expected.
3. Multiple completions can be tracked when a habit needs to be done more than once.
4. `Get last chance reminders` can remind you near the end of the day if the habit is still incomplete.
5. Today-focused views can show pending, partial, completed, or missed habit progress.

Streaks have their own strengths: they are simple, automatic, and ideal for meaningful counters that should keep moving without daily logging.

# How to add friends on Left

Friends is the social layer in Left. It is not a public profile network and it does not make all your data visible. It exists so you can intentionally share selected parts of Left with people you trust.

After you add someone as a friend, you can invite them into the parts of the app where shared time makes sense. You can share an Ahead date for a trip or event, ask them to watch a habit or streak for accountability, and receive encouragement when you complete something or need support.

To protect your privacy, adding a friend is only the first step. A friend does not automatically see all your Ahead dates, habits, or streaks. Individual items are shared later, one by one, when you choose to invite that person.

## Step 1 - Sign in

Friends requires a Left account.

If you are not signed in, open `Friends` and tap `Sign in with Apple`. You can also go to `Settings` -> `Account` and tap `Sign in`.

Signing in gives you a profile and a unique username so other Left users can find you.

## Step 2 - Open Friends

Open the `Friends` section in Left. If you are signed in, you will see your friends list and a `+` button.

Tap the `+` button to open `Add a Friend`.

## Step 3 - Share your invite link

In `Add a Friend`, Left shows `Your invite link`.

You can:

1. Copy the invite link.
2. Share it with the share button.
3. Send it to someone in Messages, Mail, or any app you use.

When someone opens your invite link, they can send or accept the friend invite from Left.

## Step 4 - Search by username

You can also add someone directly by username.

1. In `Add a Friend`, tap `Search by @username`.
2. Type their exact username.
3. If Left finds the profile, tap `Invite`.

If they already sent you a request, you may see `Accept` instead. If you already sent a request, the row will show `Pending`.

## Step 5 - Accept requests

Incoming friend requests appear in `Friend Requests`.

1. Open `Friends`.
2. If there are pending friend requests, tap the person badge button at the top.
3. In `Friend Requests`, use the `Requests` tab to accept or decline incoming requests.
4. Use the `Pending` tab to see requests you have sent.

Shared invitations are separate. If someone invites you to watch a streak or habit, or to join a Joint Ahead date, a bell badge appears at the top of `Friends`. Tap it to open `Requests`, where shared items are grouped under `Shared streaks and habits` and `Joint Ahead dates`.

## What friends let you do

After someone accepts your friend request, you can:

1. Invite them to Joint Ahead dates.
2. Ask them to watch individual habits or streaks.
3. Let them see selected progress and send encouragement.
4. See shared dates, habits, and streaks from that friend when they choose to share them with you.

Friendship alone does not make everything public. Ahead dates, habits, and streaks are shared only when you choose the item and invite that friend.

# How to organise items in Ahead or Since

Left can sort your items automatically, but automatic sorting is not always how people think about their own lives. The closest date is not always the most important date. The longest streak is not always the one you want to see first. Sometimes you want your partner, a trip, a sobriety streak, or a daily habit to stay in the same place because that order means something to you.

That is what manual organisation is for. `Ahead` lets you reorder dates you are looking forward to. `Since` lets you reorder streaks and habits. Once you save a manual order, Left can show the items in your preferred arrangement instead of sorting them by newest, alphabetical order, closest date, or longest streak.

The important detail is that your preferred order only appears when the section is sorted by `Manual`. Left switches to `Manual` when you confirm a reorganisation, but if you later choose a different sort mode, that automatic sort takes over again.

## Step 1 - Open Ahead or Since

Open the section you want to organise:

1. Use `Ahead` for dates you are counting down to.
2. Use `Since` for streaks and habits.

The same idea works in both places.

## Step 2 - Start reorganising

Long press an item, then tap `Reorganise items`.

You can do this from an Ahead date card, a streak card, or a habit card. Left enters reorganise mode and shows `Cancel` and `Confirm` at the top.

## Step 3 - Drag items into place

Drag and drop the items into the order you want.

You can move items in grid, list, or card layouts. Keep dragging until the order matches how you want the section to appear.

## Step 4 - Confirm the order

Tap `Confirm` to save the order.

If you change your mind, tap `Cancel` instead. Cancelling leaves the previous order unchanged.

## Why Manual sort matters

Left can sort items in several ways. In `Since`, sort options include `Newest`, `Oldest`, `A to Z`, `Z to A`, `Longest streak`, `Shortest streak`, and `Manual`. In `Ahead`, sort options include `Closest`, `Farthest`, `A to Z`, `Z to A`, and `Manual`.

Your custom order is visible when sorting is set to `Manual`. When you tap `Confirm` after reorganising, Left switches the section to `Manual` automatically. If you later choose another sort order, the app will show that sort instead of your custom order.

# How to be accountable to friends

Habits and streaks are easier to keep when someone else knows they matter to you. Left's accountability features are built around that idea, but they stay private and intentional: you choose the exact habit or streak, you choose the friend, and they only see what you invited them to watch.

This is different from posting progress publicly. The friend becomes a watcher for one selected `Since` item. If it is a habit, they can see whether you are completing or missing it. If it is a streak, they can see whether the streak is still going or has restarted. From there, they can send encouragement, support, or congratulations depending on the state of the item.

Use accountability for things where a little social pressure helps: staying alcohol-free, going to the gym, reading, keeping a relationship ritual, meditating, or any habit or streak where you want someone to notice whether you are still showing up.

## How accountability works

You choose a specific streak or habit and invite a friend to watch it. If they accept, they become a watcher for that item.

This is private by default. Friends do not automatically see all your habits, all your streaks, or the other friends you invited. They only see the shared item you selected.

## Step 1 - Add the friend first

Before you can ask someone to keep you accountable, they need to be your friend on Left.

Open `Friends`, tap `+`, and either share `Your invite link` or search for them with `Search by @username`. Once the request is accepted, you can share individual habits and streaks with them.

## Step 2 - Share a habit or streak

You can share when creating or editing a habit or streak.

1. Open `Since`.
2. Create a new habit or streak, or open an existing one.
3. Turn on `Friends`.
4. Tap `Add a friend`.
5. Choose the friend you want to invite.
6. Save the habit or streak.

Friends you invite may appear as `Invited` until they accept. Once accepted, they appear as `Watching`.

## Step 3 - What your friend can see

When a friend accepts the invite, they can see that shared streak or habit and its progress.

For habits, they can see whether you complete or miss the habit. For streaks, they can see whether the streak is still going or has restarted. From the shared detail screen, they can send support or congratulations when the app allows it.

This makes accountability lightweight: your friend can encourage you before a habit is complete, or send congratulations after you complete it.

## Step 4 - Remove sharing later

You can stop sharing at any time.

Open the shared habit or streak, go to the `Friends` section, and remove the friend from the item. If you are watching someone else's item, you can leave it from the shared item details.

Removing sharing stops that person from watching the item going forward.

## Notification options

Friend activity notifications are optional.

Go to `Settings` -> `Friends` to choose what can notify you:

1. `Friends requests`
2. `Ahead dates invites`
3. `Streaks & habits invites`
4. `Friends Since completions`
5. `Friends Since missed`
6. `Support from friends`

Your friend can choose their own notification settings too. They may decide to get alerts when you complete or miss shared Since activity, or turn those notifications off.

# How Joint Ahead dates work

`Ahead` is for dates you are looking forward to: trips, birthdays, deadlines, events, and anything else that gives the future a shape. Some of those dates are personal, but many of them belong to more than one person. A holiday with friends, a concert, a shared launch, or a wedding countdown is more useful when everyone can see the same date.

That is what Joint Ahead dates are for. A Joint Ahead date is an Ahead date shared with friends. You create the date, invite one or more friends, and they can accept the invitation so the date appears for them too. The creator stays the admin, which means they manage the main details and the friend list, while accepted participants can keep the same countdown in their own Left.

Joint Ahead is best when the date is shared in real life, not just in the app. It keeps the group looking at the same upcoming moment instead of everyone making separate versions of the same countdown.

## What is a Joint Ahead date?

A Joint Ahead date is an Ahead date shared with friends. The creator is the admin. The admin can edit the main details and manage who is invited. Accepted friends also get the date in their Ahead view.

Multiple friends can join the same Joint Ahead date, so everyone can count down to the same plan.

## Step 1 - Add friends

Joint Ahead works with friends, so add the people first.

Open `Friends`, tap `+`, and either share `Your invite link` or use `Search by @username`. Once someone accepts, you can invite them to an Ahead date.

## Step 2 - Create or open an Ahead date

Open `Ahead`.

You can either:

1. Tap `+` and choose `Add a date`.
2. Open an existing Ahead date and edit it.

## Step 3 - Invite friends

When creating or editing the date:

1. Turn on `Friends`.
2. Tap `Add a friend`.
3. Choose one or more friends.
4. Save the date.

Friends you invite may appear as `Invited` until they accept. The creator appears as `Creator`, accepted participants can appear as `Friend`, and your own row can appear as `You`.

## Step 4 - Friends accept the invite

When someone receives a Joint Ahead invite, a bell badge can appear in `Friends`. Tapping it opens `Requests`.

Joint date invitations appear under `Joint Ahead dates`. The invited friend can accept with the checkmark or decline with the xmark.

After accepting, the date appears for them too. They can open it from Ahead or from the friend's shared profile area.

## Managing a Joint Ahead date

The creator/admin can update the date details, add more friends, remove pending invites, or remove participants.

If you joined a date created by someone else, you can leave it with `Leave date`. Leaving removes it from your shared dates. It does not delete the date for the creator or other participants.

Joint Ahead is best for dates that belong to a group: a holiday, a concert, a shared launch, a wedding, or anything everyone wants to keep in view.

# How to restore purchases

Restoring purchases means asking the App Store to check whether your App Store account already has an active Left purchase. It is not buying again. It is a way to reconnect a previous purchase to the app when Left does not immediately detect it.

This is useful after reinstalling Left, moving to a new iPhone, restoring from backup, changing devices, or opening the app before Apple's purchase information has fully refreshed. Left already tries to detect previous purchases automatically when the app opens and when it returns to the foreground, but sometimes Apple's servers or the purchase service do not return the result right away.

If you previously purchased Left and the app does not recognise it, use `Restore purchases`. You can do it from the purchase screen itself or from `Settings` -> `Troubleshooting`.

- [What restore purchases means](#what-restore-purchases-means)
- [Restore from the purchase screen](#restore-from-the-purchase-screen)
- [Restore from Settings](#restore-from-settings)
- [Why manual restore can be needed](#why-manual-restore-can-be-needed)
- [Troubleshooting](#troubleshooting-1)

## What restore purchases means

Purchases are connected to your App Store account. Left checks that purchase information and then updates what is available inside the app.

When you tap `Restore purchases`, Left asks the App Store for your existing purchase history and refreshes the app. If the App Store says you have an active purchase, Left applies it again. If the App Store does not return an active purchase, Left will show that nothing was found.

Restoring is safe to try. It checks for an existing purchase; it does not ask you to pay again.

## Restore from the purchase screen

If you see a Left purchase screen:

1. Scroll to the bottom of the screen.
2. Tap the restore button in the footer.
3. Wait for Left to check your App Store purchase history.

If an active purchase is found, Left applies it. If nothing is found, Left will show a message saying no active purchase was detected.

## Restore from Settings

You can also restore purchases from Settings.

1. Open `Settings` in Left.
2. Tap `Troubleshooting`.
3. Tap `Restore purchases`.
4. Wait for the restore result.

This is useful if you already closed the purchase screen or want to retry later.

## Why manual restore can be needed

Left refreshes purchase status automatically when the app opens and when it returns to the foreground. Most of the time, you do not need to do anything.

Sometimes Apple's purchase servers or the purchase service do not return the purchase immediately. This can happen after reinstalling the app, changing devices, restoring from a backup, or opening the app with a poor network connection. In those cases, tapping `Restore purchases` manually asks the App Store to check again.

## Troubleshooting

- Make sure you are using the same App Store account that made the purchase.
- Check your internet connection and try again.
- If restore says no purchase was found but you believe that is wrong, wait a few minutes and retry from `Settings` -> `Troubleshooting` -> `Restore purchases`.
- If the problem continues, contact support with the purchase receipt details available in your App Store purchase history.

# How account and sync works

Left can be used locally, but an account is what lets your important data follow you beyond one device. When you sign in with Apple, Left creates an account-backed profile and can save your main app data in the cloud so it can appear again when you sign in on another iPhone or iPad.

This matters because Left contains things you probably do not want to rebuild manually: Ahead dates, habits, streaks, friends, usernames, and shared invitations. With an account, those items can sync between devices and can normally come back after reinstalling the app, as long as they had time to upload before the app was removed.

Sync is automatic, but it is not instant in every situation. It depends on network connection, cloud service availability, and whether the previous device finished syncing. Some app preferences are also device-specific, so signing in may bring back your data without making every visual setting identical on every device.

- [What syncs](#what-syncs)
- [What does not sync](#what-does-not-sync)
- [How to sign in on another device](#how-to-sign-in-on-another-device)
- [Account settings](#account-settings)

## What signing in does

You can create an account when you open Left for the first time, or later from `Settings` -> `Account` -> `Sign in`.

Left uses Sign in with Apple. After signing in, your account can store your profile and cloud-backed app data so it can appear on other devices where you sign in with the same account.

## What syncs

When you are signed in, Left saves account-backed data in the cloud, including:

1. Your name.
2. Your profile photo or profile symbol.
3. Your username.
4. Your friends and friend requests.
5. Shared friend data such as Joint Ahead dates and shared Since invitations.
6. Your Ahead dates.
7. Your habits and streaks.

Once the data is in the cloud, signing in on another device should bring it across automatically. Sync timing can vary depending on network connection, cloud service availability, and whether the app has had time to finish syncing.

## What does not sync

Some preferences are device-specific and may not follow your account.

For example, app appearance choices, local display preferences, selected layouts, and other device-level settings can remain on the device where you set them. If something looks different on a second device, check the relevant Settings section and adjust it there.

## How to sign in on another device

On the other iPhone or iPad:

1. Install Left.
2. Open the app.
3. Sign in with the same Apple account, either during first launch or from `Settings` -> `Account` -> `Sign in`.
4. Keep the app open for a moment so cloud data can load.

Your account data should appear after sync completes. If you delete the app but already had an account, signing in again should normally restore your cloud-backed data, but this cannot be guaranteed in every situation because cloud sync depends on service availability and the previous data having synced successfully.

## Account settings

Go to `Settings` -> `Account` to manage your account.

From there you can:

1. Update your name.
2. Choose a profile photo or profile symbol.
3. Set or change `Your username`.
4. `Sign in` or `Sign out`.
5. `Delete account`.

Your username is unique and lets other Left users find you. It uses lowercase letters and numbers.

## Deleting your account

`Delete account` removes your Left account and related cloud data from Left's systems. It can take time for deletion to complete across all systems.

Deleting your account does not remove data that is already stored locally on the device. If you want to remove local app data too, delete the app from the device after deleting the account.

If you only want to stop using account sync temporarily, use `Sign out` instead of deleting the account.

# How to choose your tools

Left has more tools than can comfortably fit in the main bottom menu at once. You might use `Time Left` every day, keep `Ahead` close because you are counting down to something important, and open `Since` often because you are building habits or protecting streaks. Someone else might care more about `Planner`, `Friends`, `You`, or `Left Wallpaper`.

`Choose your tools` lets you decide which tools deserve the fastest access. You can pick the tools that appear directly in the bottom menu, set the order they appear in, choose which one opens by default, and decide whether the menu shows names or just icons.

The rest of Left is still available. The tools that are not pinned stay inside the tools button at the end of the bottom menu. That button is always there, so you can keep your main menu clean without hiding the rest of the app from yourself.

## What the bottom menu is for

The bottom menu is the fastest way to move around Left. It is meant for the tools you use most often, not necessarily every tool in the app.

You can display between 2 and 4 tools in the main menu. This keeps the menu usable and prevents it from becoming crowded. The default setup starts with core tools such as `Time Left`, `Time Ahead`, and `Time Since`, but you can change that to match how you actually use Left.

Available tools include:

1. `Time Left`
2. `Planner`
3. `Time Ahead`
4. `Time Since`
5. `You`
6. `Friends`
7. `Left Wallpaper`
8. `Time Between`
9. `Games`

## Step 1 - Open Choose your tools

To customize the bottom menu:

1. Open `Settings`.
2. Tap `Choose your tools`.
3. Look at the preview at the top of the screen.

The preview shows how the bottom menu will look with your current choices. As you select, remove, reorder, or hide names, the preview helps you understand the layout before you leave Settings.

## Step 2 - Choose the tools to pin

In the tool list, tap a tool to select or deselect it.

Left lets you choose between 2 and 4 tools for the main menu. If you already have 4 selected, you need to remove one before adding another. If you only have 2 selected, Left keeps those selected so the menu does not become too empty.

Choose the tools you want to reach quickly. A good setup is usually based on your daily behaviour:

1. Pick `Time Left` if you use Left mainly as a time awareness app.
2. Pick `Time Ahead` if countdown dates are important to you.
3. Pick `Time Since` if streaks and habits are part of your routine.
4. Pick `Planner` if you use Left to understand your day.
5. Pick `Friends` if accountability and shared dates matter to you.
6. Pick `You` if you often check your profile, lifespan, and personal reflections.

There is no correct setup. The best menu is the one that removes friction from the parts of Left you actually use.

## Step 3 - Choose what opens by default

Use `Open app by default in...` to choose the tool Left should open first.

Only selected tools appear in this picker. If you want a tool to be the default, make sure it is selected in the main tool list first.

For example, if you want Left to always open on `Planner`, select `Planner` as one of your tools, then choose it in `Open app by default in...`. If you later remove that tool from the menu, Left will choose another selected tool as the default.

## Step 4 - Reorder your tools

The order of selected tools controls how they appear in the bottom menu.

To change it:

1. Tap `Reorder` in the top-right.
2. Drag the selected tools into the order you want.
3. Tap `Done`.

The footer changes to `Drag to change the order. Selected tools keep that order in the main menu.` while you are reordering. Only the selected tools are shown in this mode, so you can focus on the tools that will actually appear in the bottom menu.

## Step 5 - Hide tool names if you prefer icons

Turn on `Hide names` if you want a cleaner bottom menu with icons only.

This is useful if you already know the icons or prefer a more minimal layout. If you want the menu to be easier to read at a glance, leave names visible.

You can switch this setting back at any time.

## Where the rest of the tools go

Tools that are not selected do not disappear. They move behind the tools button at the end of the bottom menu.

That button uses the tools icon and always remains available. Tap it when you want to open a tool that is not pinned in your main menu. This is the balance Left is aiming for: your favourite tools stay one tap away, and everything else remains accessible without crowding the interface.

Changes save automatically when you leave `Choose your tools`.

# How to set up your lifespan

Your lifespan is one of the most important personal settings in Left. It affects how the app shows your life left, how your profile feels, and how several reflective views inside Left understand your age and time remaining.

Left does not treat lifespan as an exact prediction. It is an estimate used to make time visible. The point is not to tell you the exact date of anything. The point is to give your life view a scale, so years, months, weeks, profile stats, and visual reflections can be shown in a way that feels personal instead of generic.

You can set the basic lifespan settings from `Settings` -> `Account` -> `Your lifespan`. From there, you can choose your birthday, decide how your life should appear in `Time Left`, calculate an estimated lifespan from personal factors, or set a manual lifespan if you prefer to choose the number yourself.

## What lifespan is used for

Left uses your lifespan setting anywhere the app needs to understand your life as a timeline.

That includes:

1. The `Your life` view inside `Time Left`.
2. Life widgets and life-based visualizations.
3. Your profile and `You` screen.
4. Lifespan progress.
5. Life stages and personal reflection charts.
6. Sleep, awake, activity, and relationship-style lifespan views.

If your birthday or lifespan is missing, some of those views cannot be personalised properly. Once they are set, Left can show your life in years, months, or weeks and calculate how much of that timeline has already passed.

## Step 1 - Open Your lifespan

To find the setting:

1. Open `Settings`.
2. Tap `Account`.
3. Tap `Your lifespan`.

This opens the lifespan settings screen. The title at the top is `Your lifespan`.

## Step 2 - Set your birthday

In `Your lifespan`, set `Birthday`.

Your birthday tells Left where your personal timeline starts. Without it, Left cannot calculate your current age or show life progress accurately.

The birthday picker only allows dates up to today. Choose your actual birthday if you want the most accurate reflections in `Time Left`, widgets, and the `You` screen.

## Step 3 - Choose how to see your life

Use `See your life in...` to choose how the life view is displayed.

You can choose:

1. `Years`
2. `Months`
3. `Weeks`

`Years` gives the simplest view. `Months` makes the timeline feel more detailed. `Weeks` makes life feel more granular and can be more confronting because it shows many more individual units.

You can also turn on `Hide Life in Left view` if you do not want the life view to appear inside the main `Time Left` view. This does not delete your lifespan settings. It only hides that life section from the Left view.

## Step 4 - Calculate your lifespan

Tap `Calculate your lifespan` to open the lifespan calculator.

The calculator asks for broad factors that can influence life expectancy, including:

1. `Gender`
2. `Country of birth`
3. `Exercise`
4. `Regular smoker`
5. `Alcohol consumption`
6. `Diet type`
7. `Sleep hours`
8. `Stress level`

Left uses these factors together to estimate a lifespan. It does not simply add or subtract a fixed number of years for each answer, and the app should not be read like a medical assessment. It is a general estimate based on the information you provide and broad statistical patterns.

At the top, Left shows `Your life expectancy is` followed by an approximate number of years. When available, you can use the `Calculate` button to generate an estimate from the selected factors.

## Step 5 - Set your lifespan manually

If you do not want Left to estimate your lifespan from factors, you can choose `Set your lifespan manually`.

Manual lifespan means you choose the number yourself instead of letting Left calculate it. This can be useful if you already have a number you want to use, if you prefer a symbolic lifespan, or if you want the app's life views to match a personal belief rather than a statistical estimate.

Manual lifespan changes the number directly, so use it intentionally. It will affect how Left scales your life views, widgets, profile reflections, and time-left calculations.

## Important notes

Your lifespan in Left is an approximation. It is meant for visual reflection, not medical advice, financial planning, or a guarantee about the future.

Changing your lifespan changes how life-based views are scaled. If you increase or decrease the lifespan value, your life progress, years left, months left, weeks left, widgets, and profile reflections may change.

The setting is personal. Use a number that makes Left useful for you: realistic enough to make the app meaningful, but not something you treat as a certainty.
