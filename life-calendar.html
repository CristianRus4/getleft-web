A life calendar for your iPhone: your life in weeks

A life calendar draws a whole lifetime as a grid, one box for every week, and fills in the weeks you have already lived. It sounds stark, and it is meant to be clarifying. What a life calendar is

Take an ordinary lifespan, say 80 years, and draw it as rows of 52 boxes, one row per year. That is about 4,160 weeks, and it fits on a single page. Shade in the weeks you have lived, and what is left becomes something you can actually see. The format was made popular by Tim Urban’s "Your Life in Weeks" essay on Wait But Why, and it echoes a much older idea: memento mori, the Stoic habit of remembering that time is limited so you spend it on purpose.

A paper chart is static. In Left the grid updates on its own: each week, one more box dims.

Why people keep one

Most people do not use a life calendar to feel gloomy. They use it to get perspective. Seeing that a week is a small but visible part of the whole makes it easier to say no to things that do not matter and yes to the trip, the call or the project you keep postponing. It also makes ordinary weeks feel less disposable.

If weeks feel too intense, switch the view to months or years for a gentler picture, or hide the life view from the main screen entirely. The point is awareness, not pressure.

How to set up your life calendar in Left

  1. Open Left, go to Settings and open Your lifespan.
  2. Set your Birthday. It tells Left where your timeline starts; without it, Left cannot show life progress.
  3. Under See your life in..., choose Weeks. Months and Years are there too.
  4. Go back to Time Left and switch to the life view, labelled Your life (or your name, if you have added one). You will see the grid, the weeks you have left, and your current year and age.
  5. Optional: turn on Hide Life in Left view if you would rather not see it every time you open the app. Your settings are kept.

About the lifespan number

Left uses an 80-year lifespan by default. With Left Premium you can change it: Calculate your lifespan estimates a number from broad factors such as country of birth, exercise, smoking, alcohol, diet, sleep and stress, and Set your lifespan manually lets you choose the number yourself. Either way it is an approximation for reflection, not a medical prediction. Changing it rescales every life view, widget and wallpaper. The lifespan guide covers every option.

Questions

What is a life calendar?

A life calendar is a grid of every week in a typical lifespan, usually 52 boxes per row and one row per year, with the weeks you have lived filled in. It makes the time you have left visible at a glance.

How many weeks are in a lifetime?

An 80-year life is about 4,160 weeks (80 × 52). Left uses your birthday and lifespan to work out how many of those you have lived and how many are left.

Is Left’s life calendar free?

Yes. The life view, the Life and You widgets and the life wallpaper are free with Left’s 80-year default lifespan. Setting a custom lifespan needs Left Premium.

Can I see my life in weeks on my Lock Screen?

Yes. Left Wallpaper has a Life mode that draws your life in weeks, months or years as your Lock Screen wallpaper, and the You widget can show your weeks left as a number on the Lock Screen.

Is the lifespan a prediction?

No. Whether you use the 80-year default, the calculator or a number you choose, it is an approximation for reflection, not medical advice or a forecast.
