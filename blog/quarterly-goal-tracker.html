Quarterly Goal Tracker: How to Set and Track 90-Day Goals on iPhone

Updated November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Yearly goals have a timing problem. In January, December feels so far away that nothing is urgent, so the first months drift. By October, it's too late to catch up. A quarter fixes that. Thirteen weeks is long enough to finish something real and short enough that week 2 already matters.

That's why companies plan in quarters, and why it works just as well for personal goals. This guide covers how to choose quarterly goals, how to break them into weekly numbers you can actually track, and how to set up a quarterly goal tracker on your iPhone that shows both your progress and how much of the quarter is left.

Why 90 days works

  • The deadline is always close. In a quarter, there's no "I'll start next month": next month is a third of the time.
  • Mistakes are cheap. If a goal was wrong, you lose 13 weeks, not a year, and you get a clean restart four times a year.
  • It matches real life. Seasons, school terms, work cycles and training blocks often run about three months.
  • The pace is visible. At week 6 you should be close to halfway. If you're at 20%, you know now, not in December.

How to set quarterly goals

  1. Start from the year. Write down the 2 or 3 things you want by year end. Each quarter should move one of them forward.
  2. Pick no more than three goals. One is often better. Every extra goal halves the attention each one gets.
  3. Give each goal a number. "Get fitter" becomes "run 150 km". "Grow the side project" becomes "10 paying customers". If you can't count it, you can't track it. SMART goals help here.
  4. Add the habit that gets you there. The number is the outcome; the habit is what you do each week. 150 km needs about three runs a week.
  5. Write the weekly target. Divide the number by 13. 150 km is about 11.5 km a week. That's the number to check every Sunday.

What a quarterly goal tracker needs to show

A spreadsheet can hold the numbers, but you won't open it on a Tuesday. A useful tracker shows four things without you looking for them:

  • Progress: where you are against the target, as a number and a percentage.
  • Time left: how many days remain in the quarter.
  • Pace: whether you're ahead or behind for the date.
  • The weekly habit: whether you did this week's runs, transfers or reading.

Progress without time left is misleading: 50% sounds good until you see 80% of the quarter has gone.

Set up a quarterly goal tracker on iPhone with Left

Left, an iPhone app we make, covers all four with three of its features. You can rebuild the same idea with other apps; the structure is what matters.

1. Each goal as an Aim with the quarter's deadline

2. The weekly habit

In Since, create a habit with a weekly period and a count: "Run, 3 times a week". Weekly habits can be done on any days in the week, which suits real schedules better than fixed days.

In Ahead, add the quarter's last day ("Q1 ends"). Add it as a widget next to your Aim widgets, so "38 days left" and "61% done" sit side by side. The quarter countdown calculator shows how much of the current quarter is left right now.

Track your quarterly goals

The weekly review (10 minutes, Sunday)

  1. Open each Aim. Ahead, on track or behind?
  2. Check the weekly habit: did you hit the count?
  3. If you're behind, pick one fix for the coming week: an extra session, a smaller weekly target, or dropping a goal.
  4. Glance at the countdown. Thirteen weeks becomes twelve faster than it sounds.

Weekly reviews are where quarterly goals are won. A goal that's 10% behind in week 4 is easy to recover; in week 11 it isn't. For more on keeping going when motivation dips, read how to stay consistent with goals.

At the end of the quarter

  • Score honestly. 70% of an ambitious goal is often a better quarter than 100% of an easy one.
  • Keep or archive. Archive Aims you've finished or dropped. If a goal continues, restart the Aim for the new quarter.
  • Keep the habit. The habit usually outlasts the goal. Running three times a week doesn't need to stop because the 150 km are done.
  • Set the next three. Do it before the new quarter starts, not in week 2.

Questions

What is a quarterly goal?

A goal you plan to finish within one quarter, about 13 weeks or 90 days. Quarters are long enough to achieve something meaningful and short enough that the deadline feels real from the first week.

How many quarterly goals should I set?

One to three. Each extra goal splits your attention, and three is usually the most people can make steady weekly progress on.

How do I track quarterly goals on iPhone?

Turn each goal into a number with a deadline on the quarter's last day, track the weekly habit that gets you there, and keep a countdown to quarter end visible. In Left, that's an Aim, a weekly habit and an Ahead countdown, each available as a widget.

What if I fall behind in the middle of the quarter?

Check your pace weekly so you notice early. Then make one change: add a session, lower the target to something realistic, or drop a goal to protect the others.

Should quarterly goals follow the calendar quarters?

They don't have to. Calendar quarters (January to March and so on) are easy to remember, but any 13-week block works, such as a training cycle or a school term.
