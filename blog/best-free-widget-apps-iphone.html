10 Best Free Widget Apps for iPhone (2026)

Updated October 20, 2026 · 4 min read

"Free" widget apps come in three flavors: genuinely free (Apple builds them), freemium with a useful free tier, and "free" with a paywall around every widget that's worth using. This list separates the three honestly. We graded each app by what you can actually do without paying - not what's advertised.

Ten apps below. Left sits at the top because its Time Left widgets work across the Home Screen, Lock Screen and StandBy, and it is free to download. The other nine fill different specialties.

1. Left - free time-left widgets, optional Premium

Left is free to download. For free you get the Time Left views, the widgets, Left Wallpaper, and two each of countdowns (Ahead dates), habits, streaks and aims, counted as lifetime creations. Left Premium unlocks unlimited items, Planner, fasting, Apple Health tracking, friends and games. Prices are shown in the app before you buy.

Best for: people who want time left, widgets, habits, streaks, countdowns, and Planner in one place.

2. Widgetsmith - freemium, decent free tier

Free tier: typographic clock, date, weather widgets in limited styles. Premium unlocks more fonts and custom photos.

Best for: people who want clean date/clock widgets and don't need data. Cons: the best designs are paywalled.

3. Color Widgets - freemium with ads

Free tier: thousands of pre-made aesthetic widgets. Ads in the free tier; subscription removes them.

Best for: aesthetic Home Screens with low effort. Cons: ads.

4. Widgy - free with optional pro

Free tier: install and use community-made widgets. Pro tier unlocks the editor for building your own.

Best for: using community designs without building. Cons: building requires paying.

5. Launcher - freemium

Free tier: basic launcher widgets for actions and contacts. Pro tier adds more actions and Shortcuts integration.

Best for: anyone who repeats the same phone actions and wants a one-tap shortcut. Cons: real power requires Pro.

6. Widgetable - freemium

Free tier: shared social widgets with a partner or friend group. Some widgets paywalled.

Best for: couples and friend groups wanting shared widgets. Cons: the most fun social widgets are Pro.

7. Top Widgets+ - free

Free with optional purchases. Variety of weather, time, photo widgets. Quality varies widget to widget.

Best for: trying lots of widget styles cheaply. Cons: inconsistent quality.

8. Photo Widget - free

Free photo-rotation widget. Pick photos, set timing, done. Premium adds advanced layouts.

Best for: rotating personal photos on the Home Screen. Cons: simple by design.

9. ScreenKit - freemium with trial

Trial period for full themes; subscription after. Limited free coverage.

Best for: trying out themed Home Screens. Cons: not really free past the trial.

10. Apple's built-in widgets - free

Honestly underrated. Calendar, Weather, Reminders, Photos, Activity, Battery, Find My, Music. Free, well-designed, reliable. Always worth a slot or two on the Home Screen.

Best for: anyone. Cons: limited customization.

What "free" actually means in widget apps

  • Apple's built-ins: genuinely free, no caveats.
  • Freemium: useful free tier, paid for deeper features (Left, Widgetsmith).
  • Ad-supported free: functional but cluttered (Color Widgets, Top Widgets).
  • Trial / paywall-everywhere: "free download" but you'll pay quickly (ScreenKit, several aesthetic apps).

Knowing which category an app is in before installing saves the disappointment of finding the feature you wanted is paywalled.

A working free stack

  • Apple's built-ins for Calendar, Weather, Battery.
  • Left for free Time Left widgets.
  • Widgetsmith's free tier for a designed typographic clock or date.
  • Apple Photos widget for the personal photo touch.

Four widget sources, zero subscriptions, complete Home Screen coverage.

When to pay

Honest reasons to upgrade:

  • The Premium features would genuinely help you keep habits, streaks, dates, or your day in view.
  • A specific paid feature in another app solves a problem you have today, not someday.
  • You've used the free version for a month and want to support the developer.

Bad reasons:

  • "Pro looks more polished in screenshots." (You won't use the polish.)
  • "I might use this feature." (You won't.)
