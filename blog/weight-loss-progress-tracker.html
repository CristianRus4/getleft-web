How to Set Up a Weight Loss Progress Tracker on iPhone That Tracks the Trend

Updated November 12, 2026 · 8 min read

You step on the scale on Monday and you're down. Tuesday you're up by more than you lost. Nothing changed except dinner was saltier and you slept badly. If your tracker is a list of daily numbers, that Tuesday reading feels like failure, and a few of those in a row is how people quit.

A good weight loss progress tracker smooths out that noise so you see the real direction, and puts the outcome next to the few behaviors you control on any given day. Here's what to track, how often to weigh, how to read a plateau calmly, and a full setup on iPhone and Apple Watch.

One note first. This is about tracking, not about what to eat or how much to lose. A doctor or dietitian is the right person for a plan, especially if you have a health condition or take medication. And if tracking your weight starts to feel unhealthy, it's fine to stop, or to track only the habits.

Why the daily number misleads you

Body weight moves around within a day and from one day to the next for reasons that have little to do with fat. Water is the big one. A salty meal, more carbohydrates than usual, a hard workout, a late dinner, a hot day or a different point in a menstrual cycle can all shift the scale noticeably overnight. So can simply weighing at a different time.

None of that is progress or setback. It's noise on top of a slower signal. The fix is to keep the conditions the same and look at averages.

What to track

A useful tracker has four parts. Each one covers a gap in the others.

The weight trend

Once a week, average that week's weigh-ins and compare with last week's average. That one comparison tells you more than seven separate numbers.

Measurements

A tape measure around the waist, at the same spot each time, is a good second opinion. Clothes fitting differently counts too.

Behavior habits

This is the part most trackers skip, and it's the part that does the work. Pick two or three behaviors you can complete or miss on a given day, and that a professional you trust would agree with. For example:

  • Walk 20 minutes, or hit a daily step goal
  • Protein at breakfast
  • Kitchen closed after 8 pm
  • In bed by 11

Keep it to three at most. When the weekly average stalls, a short list makes it obvious which habit slipped.

Non-scale wins

Stairs feel easier. A belt goes in a notch. Write these down with a date. On a frustrating week they are evidence that something is changing.

How often to weigh

There are two sensible options, and both work if you keep the conditions the same.

  • Daily, then average. More readings, steadier average. Suits people who can see a number and shrug.
  • Once or twice a week. Less exposure to noise. Suits people who find daily numbers stressful.

Whichever you choose, weigh at the same time of day, ideally in the morning after the bathroom and before eating or drinking, in similar clothes, on the same scale on a hard floor.

How to read a plateau without panicking

Sooner or later the weekly average stops moving for a week or two. Before you change anything, go through this in order:

  1. Check the habits first. Did you hit your walks, breakfast and evening cutoff most days? If they slipped, that's your answer, and the fix is to get back to them, not to add new rules.
  2. Check the conditions. Different weigh-in times, travel, a new workout, poor sleep, a stressful week or a different point in a cycle can all hold the scale up for a while.
  3. Check the other signals. If measurements or clothes are still moving the right way, the scale may be lagging. If the habits are steady, a couple of flat weeks is normal.
  4. Ask for help if it lasts. If the trend stays flat for a long time, or you're tempted to try something drastic, talk to your doctor or a dietitian rather than cutting harder on your own.

The habit list is what makes this calm: you can see in ten seconds whether the plan stalled or you did.

The Apple Health app as your data hub

On iPhone, the Health app is the natural home for raw weight data. You can add weight entries by hand under Body Measurements, and many smart scales write each weigh-in to Health automatically once you connect their app. Health then charts your weight by week, month and year, which is a quick way to see the long-term direction.

Health also collects steps, workouts and sleep from your iPhone and Apple Watch, so some of the habits above are already being measured.

Setting up the whole system in Left

1. An Aim for the weight trend

An Aim is a number goal that counts up or down. For weight, use a count-down Aim and log the trend, not every weigh-in:

  1. Open Since, tap + and choose an Aim. Pick count down.
  2. Set the unit (kg or lb), the starting number and the target.
  3. Set the default log amount to a small step, such as 0.5 kg or 1 lb. One tap logs it.
  4. Add a deadline only if you want one. Left will then show whether you're on track, ahead or behind. If a deadline makes you anxious, leave it off.

Each week, compare your weekly average with the last one you logged. If it has dropped by your step, tap once. If it went up, leave it, or correct the entry. Left keeps a progress history, so you can see every step and fix a mistake later. The Aim only moves when the trend does.

2. Two or three habits

Add a Habit for each behavior. Daily habits can run every day, weekdays, specific days or a set number of days per week, so "Walk 20 minutes, 5 days a week" is easy to express. Each habit shows its streak, longest streak, a completion grid and habit strength, which is more forgiving than a streak that drops to zero after one missed day.

Use Get reminders for a 7 pm walk nudge, or Get a persistent reminder for the habit you tend to forget.

With Left Premium, Apple Health automatic tracking can complete a habit like "8,000 steps" on its own, using the steps your iPhone or Apple Watch already counts.

3. A streak for what you're cutting out

If you're stopping something, such as soda, late-night snacking or takeout on weeknights, a Streak fits better than a habit. It counts up from Started on... without any logging. If you slip, tap Restart; Left keeps your longest and average streak, so a restart doesn't erase the record.

  • Widgets: an Aim widget on the Home Screen shows the current number, target and a progress bar. Habits list and Streak widgets sit beside it, and several work on the Lock Screen.
  • Apple Watch: the Aims screen lets you log and unlog progress from your wrist, and the Habits screen shows what's due today and logs it with one tap. You choose which screens appear from the iPhone app.
  • Notes: add dated notes to the Aim for non-scale wins and for odd weeks ("travel, weighed at night").

Other ways to track weight loss

  • The Health app alone: free and already on your iPhone. Good for storing weigh-ins and seeing long-term charts, but it doesn't pair the number with your habits.
  • Dedicated weight apps: built around charts and trend lines for weight. A good fit if the number is all you want to follow.
  • Your smart scale's app: weigh-ins arrive without typing, but most are built around the scale's own data.
  • A notebook or spreadsheet: flexible and private, but no reminders, and averaging is on you.

Whichever you use, weigh under the same conditions, judge the weekly trend, and put most of your attention on the habits you can do today. For the weeks when motivation fades, how to stay consistent with goals has more ideas.

Questions

What is the best way to track weight loss progress on iPhone?

Weigh at the same time of day, store weigh-ins in the Health app, and judge progress by your weekly average rather than daily readings. Pair that trend with two or three habits you control, such as daily walks, tracked in an app like Left.

Why does my weight go up and down every day?

Day-to-day changes are mostly water, influenced by salt, carbohydrates, exercise, sleep, meal timing and hormonal cycles. Weighing under the same conditions and averaging over a week shows the real direction.

How often should I weigh myself?

Either daily with a weekly average, or once or twice a week on the same day and time. Daily readings give a steadier average; weekly weigh-ins suit people who find daily numbers stressful.

Can Left read my weight from Apple Health?

Yes, with Left Premium. Set a count-down Aim's tracking to Apple Health and choose body mass, and progress fills from your weigh-ins in Health. On the free plan, you log the Aim yourself.

What should I do when my weight loss stalls?

Check whether your habits slipped, whether your weigh-in conditions changed, and whether measurements are still moving. If the habits are steady, give it time; if a plateau lasts, talk to a doctor or dietitian.

Is it okay to stop weighing myself?

Yes. If tracking your weight feels unhealthy, you can stop and track only the habits, or take a break entirely. A doctor or dietitian can help you find an approach that suits you.
