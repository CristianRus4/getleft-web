When to break your fast

The end of your fasting window is the moment your fast duration elapses from the start time. For a 16-hour fast starting at 8:00 PM, the eating window opens at 12:00 PM the next day.

Many people find it easier to break longer fasts (24+ hours) gradually: start with something light and easy to digest - fruit, broth, or yoghurt - before moving to a full meal. For standard 16:8 or 18:6 fasts, normal meals are fine.

What counts as "fast start"?

Your fast starts the moment you finish your last caloric intake - not when you decide to start fasting. If your last meal ended at 7:45 PM, that's your start time, not 8:00 PM. For precision, enter the actual time you finished eating.

How the end time is worked out

The calculator adds your fast duration, in hours and minutes, to the date and time your fast started. It counts real elapsed time, then shows the result in your device's local time, together with a live countdown and how much of the fast is done.

A worked example: your last meal ended at 7:45 PM on Tuesday, November 10, 2026. Common fasts would end at:

  • 16 hours (16:8) - 11:45 AM on Wednesday, November 11
  • 18 hours (18:6) - 1:45 PM on Wednesday
  • 20 hours (20:4) - 3:45 PM on Wednesday
  • 24 hours - 7:45 PM on Wednesday
  • 36 hours - 7:45 AM on Thursday, November 12

In 16:8 and 18:6, the first number is the hours of fasting and the second the hours of the eating window, so together they make 24. OMAD, one meal a day, usually means a fast of about 23 hours. A 24-hour fast runs from one meal to the same meal the next day.

Midnight, clock changes and travel

The start field includes a date, so a fast that crosses midnight needs nothing special: enter last night's date and time and the calculator carries the end into today or tomorrow.

Because the calculator adds real hours, a fast that crosses a daylight saving change ends at a different clock time than you might expect. In the US, clocks go forward on Sunday, March 14, 2027: a 16-hour fast that starts at 8:00 PM on the Saturday ends at 1:00 PM, not 12:00 PM. In the UK, the same happens on Sunday, March 28, 2027. When the clocks go back in the autumn, the same fast ends at 11:00 AM.

If you fly across time zones during a fast, enter the start time as it was in the time zone your phone is now set to, so the end time comes out right.

This page does not decide what breaks a fast or which length suits you. It only works out times.

Is fasting right for you?

Fasting is not suitable for everyone. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, under 18, have diabetes or another medical condition, have had an eating disorder, or take regular medication, talk to a doctor before you fast. Mayo Clinic has an overview of intermittent fasting. This calculator only works out times; it does not give medical advice.

Questions

When does a 16-hour fast end if I stop eating at 8 PM?

At 12:00 PM the next day. An 18-hour fast from 8:00 PM ends at 2:00 PM, and a 20-hour fast at 4:00 PM.

When does a 24-hour fast end?

At the same clock time the next day, for example 7:45 PM to 7:45 PM. On the night the clocks change, it ends an hour earlier or later on the clock.

When does my fast start?

When you finish your last meal or snack, not when you decide to start. If you finished eating at 7:45 PM, enter 7:45 PM.

What is the difference between 16:8 and 18:6?

16:8 is 16 hours of fasting with an 8-hour eating window. 18:6 is 18 hours of fasting with a 6-hour window.

Does the calculator use my time zone?

Yes. It reads the start time in your device's local time zone and shows the end time in the same zone.

Can I track my fast in Left?

Yes. You can add the end time as an Ahead countdown with a Lock Screen widget. Left also has a Fasting timer with a Live Activity on the Lock Screen and Apple Watch, which is part of Left Premium.
