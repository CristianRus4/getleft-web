When is Ramadan?

Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic calendar, the month in which Muslims fast from dawn to sunset. In 2027 the first day of fasting is expected on Monday, February 8, 2027, with the first Tarawih prayers on the evening before, Sunday, February 7, 2027.

Ramadan lasts 29 or 30 days and ends with Eid al-Fitr, the festival of breaking the fast, expected on Tuesday, March 9, 2027. The days until Eid page counts down to it.

Why the start depends on the moon

The Islamic calendar is purely lunar. Each month begins with the first sighting of the new crescent moon, so a year has 354 or 355 days, about 11 days fewer than the Gregorian year. Ramadan therefore starts about 11 days earlier every year and passes through every season in about 33 years. In 2030 it begins twice, on January 5 and on December 26.

Some countries wait for the crescent to be seen, locally or in Saudi Arabia, while others follow an astronomical calculation. That is why the first day of Ramadan can differ by a day from one country, or one community, to another.

How this countdown works

The dates are the expected ones from the Umm al-Qura calendar, the official calendar of Saudi Arabia. The page counts down to the start of the first day of fasting in your own time zone, using your device’s clock. The fast itself begins at dawn, so check the exact time of suhoor and iftar with your local mosque.

Once Ramadan starts, the counter shows the day of Ramadan and the line under it shows when Eid al-Fitr is expected. If the moon is sighted a day earlier or later where you live, count from your local first day.

The days of Ramadan

Each day of fasting begins with suhoor, a meal before dawn, and ends at sunset with iftar, often shared with family and friends. In the evenings many people attend Tarawih, special night prayers held throughout the month.

The last ten nights are the most important part of Ramadan. Among them is Laylat al-Qadr, the Night of Power, which is sought especially on the odd-numbered nights. The list in the countdown shows when the last ten nights are expected to begin.

Questions

When does Ramadan 2027 start?

The first day of fasting is expected on Monday, February 8, 2027, with the first Tarawih prayers on the evening of Sunday, February 7, 2027. The exact day depends on the sighting of the new moon and can differ by a day between countries.

Why does Ramadan start on a different date each year?

Ramadan follows the Islamic lunar calendar, whose year is about 11 days shorter than the Gregorian year. Each year Ramadan therefore begins about 11 days earlier than the year before.

How long does Ramadan last?

29 or 30 days. The month ends when the next new moon is sighted, or after 30 days if it is not. Ramadan 2027 is expected to last 29 days, with Eid al-Fitr on Tuesday, March 9, 2027.

Why are there two Ramadans in 2030?

Because the Islamic year is about 11 days shorter than the Gregorian year, Ramadan sometimes begins twice in the same Gregorian year. In 2030 it is expected to begin on January 5 and again on December 26.
