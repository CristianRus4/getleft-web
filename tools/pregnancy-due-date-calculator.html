How the due date is calculated

The standard method (Naegele's Rule) adds 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last menstrual period. Doctors and midwives use the same rule for a first estimate, before a dating scan. If your cycle is not 28 days long, enter your usual cycle length: the calculator moves the date by the difference (for a 32-day cycle, four days later).

The 40-week count starts from your LMP - even though conception typically happens around week 2. So your "gestational age" as measured medically is always about 2 weeks ahead of your embryonic age.

Four ways to date a pregnancy

Choose the method that matches what you know. All four give the same kind of answer: a due date 40 weeks after the start of the pregnancy count, your week and day today, and the time to go.

  • Last period: the first day of your last period plus 280 days, adjusted for your cycle length.
  • Conception: the conception date plus 266 days (38 weeks). Use it if you know when you ovulated or conceived.
  • Dating scan: enter the scan date and the weeks and days the sonographer gave you. The calculator counts back to the start of the pregnancy and forward 40 weeks.
  • IVF transfer: the transfer date plus 266 days, minus the embryo's age. A day-5 blastocyst gives a due date 261 days after transfer, a day-3 embryo 263 days.

Dating by scan versus by last period

The last-period method assumes you ovulated 14 days after your period started. Many people don't: cycles vary, and some don't know the exact date of their last period. A scan measures the baby instead. Early in pregnancy babies grow at a very similar rate, so the crown-rump length measured between about 10 and 14 weeks dates a pregnancy to within about a week.

That is why, when the two disagree, the scan usually wins. The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists advises changing the due date to the scan's date when a first-trimester scan differs from the last-period date by more than 7 days (more than 5 days before 9 weeks). Later scans are less precise for dating, because babies' sizes spread out as they grow. Once your midwife or doctor has set your due date, use the scan method with their numbers and this page will agree with them.

Trimesters

  • First trimester: Weeks 1–12 (LMP to ~84 days)
  • Second trimester: Weeks 13–26 (~85 to ~182 days)
  • Third trimester: Weeks 27–40 (~183 days to due date)

How accurate is this date?

Only about 4% of babies are born on their exact due date. The estimated due date (EDD) is the midpoint of a normal delivery window that typically spans from 38 to 42 weeks. A dating scan in the first trimester gives a more accurate date, particularly when your cycle length differs from the standard 28-day assumption or you are unsure of your last period.

Questions

How accurate is a due date?

It is an estimate, not a deadline. Only about 4 in 100 babies arrive on the exact day; most are born in the weeks either side of it. A first-trimester dating scan gives the most reliable date.

What if my cycle is irregular?

Naegele's rule assumes ovulation about 14 days after your period started, so an irregular cycle makes the last-period method less reliable. Use the scan method once you have a dating scan, or ask your midwife or doctor.

When is the dating scan?

In the UK the NHS offers it at around 10 to 14 weeks. Early scans measure the baby from head to bottom (the crown-rump length), which predicts the due date well because babies grow at a similar rate early on.

How many weeks is full term?

Full term is 39 weeks 0 days to 40 weeks 6 days. Babies born from 37 weeks are early term, at 41 weeks late term, and from 42 weeks post-term.

How is an IVF due date calculated?

From the transfer date: conception is counted as the day the embryo was created, so a day-5 blastocyst transferred on 1 March gives a due date 261 days later, on 17 November (in a non-leap year).

Does the calculator store my dates?

No. The calculation runs in your browser and nothing you enter is sent anywhere.
