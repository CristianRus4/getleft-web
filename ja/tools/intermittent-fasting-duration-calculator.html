Common fasting protocols

  • 16:8 — Fast for 16 hours, eat within an 8-hour window. The most popular protocol; compatible with most lifestyles when the eating window is set at noon–8 PM.
  • 18:6 — A stricter variant; 18-hour fast leaves a 6-hour eating window. Popular with experienced IF practitioners.
  • 20:4 (Warrior Diet) — 20-hour fast with a 4-hour evening eating window. Often involves one large meal.
  • OMAD (One Meal A Day) — A 23–24 hour fast. Effective for weight loss but requires attention to nutrient density in the single meal.
  • 36 hours — An extended fast, typically done once or twice per week (e.g., 5:2 protocol). Consult a doctor before attempting.

What breaks a fast?

Caloric intake breaks a fast. Water, black coffee, and plain tea are generally considered fast-safe. Milk, cream, sugar, or any caloric addition ends the fast. Some practitioners also allow zero-calorie electrolytes for extended fasts.
